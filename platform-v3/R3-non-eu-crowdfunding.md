Research date: 2026-08-01. Prepared for the Consulting24 crowdfunding-license service line (Estonia ECSPR anchor, EUR 37,000 full application package). All hard facts carry inline source URLs; anything not confirmed against a current source is marked UNVERIFIED.

## 0. REGULATOR VERIFICATION FLAG (must fix in the offer docx before anything ships)

The commercial offer document ("Crowdfunding License Setup Offer", X24Consulting OÜ) repeatedly names the **Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU)** as the licensing authority. **This is wrong for ECSPR crowdfunding.** Estonian crowdfunding service provider authorizations under Regulation (EU) 2020/1503 are issued by **Finantsinspektsioon (the Estonian Financial Supervision Authority / Estonian FSA)** — confirmed against the live Finantsinspektsioon register in the first-party research (R1/R2/R6, verified Feb–Aug 2026: Estateguru licensed by Finantsinspektsioon May 2023; Crowdestate licence withdrawn by Finantsinspektsioon 13 Apr 2026; EUR 1,000 state fee; 25-working-day completeness check + 3-month assessment; applications via the FI portal mandatory since 18 Mar 2026). The FIU (Rahapesu Andmebüroo) licenses VASPs/crypto — the audience Consulting24 knows — but it has no role in ECSPR authorization. **Every page, offer and email in the crowdfunding cluster must say Finantsinspektsioon / Estonian FSA. This report uses only Finantsinspektsioon.** The docx language is likely a copy-paste from the crypto-license offer template and should be corrected; leaving "FIU" in a published offer would be an instant credibility kill in front of anyone who has read ECSPR Art. 12.

## 0.1 Estonia baseline used for every comparison below

- Regulator: Finantsinspektsioon; ECSPR (EU 2020/1503) authorization; state fee EUR 1,000; statutory 25-working-day completeness check + 3-month assessment (realistic ~3–6 months end-to-end per the offer).
- Own funds: higher of EUR 25,000 or 1/4 of fixed overheads — coverable by share capital OR an insurance policy (offer: ~EUR 1,000–1,500/month insurance replacing own funds).
- Consulting24 all-in: EUR 37,000 (+EUR 3,000 optional business plan); documentation already passed two rounds of regulator review.
- The prize: **one authorization passports to the entire EU single market (27 member states) via ECSPR Art. 18 notification** (Consulting24 already holds a T9 Art-18 cross-border notification template). Issuer cap EUR 5M per offer per 12 months.
- The framing for every "Estonia vs X" page: **EU-passport-via-Estonia vs domestic-market access.** No non-EU license gives EU access; no EU license gives domestic non-EU access. They are complements, not substitutes — which is exactly why comparison pages convert (many founders genuinely don't know this).

## 1. UNITED KINGDOM — FCA

**License/permission type & regulator.** No single "crowdfunding license." The FCA authorizes two routes ([FCA loan-based crowdfunding summary](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/loan-based-crowdfunding-platforms-summary-our-rules)):
- **Loan-based (P2P):** Part 4A permission for *operating an electronic system in relation to lending* (RAO Art. 36H).
- **Investment-based:** permissions such as *arranging deals in investments* / dealing as agent (equity/debt securities platforms), per [FCA PS19/14](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/ps19-14-loan-based-peer-to-peer-investment-based-crowdfunding-platforms-feedback-final-rules).

**Post-2023 financial-promotions regime (the moat nobody prices in).** Since 1 Dec 2022 / 1 Feb 2023 (PS22/10), P2P agreements and non-readily-realisable securities are "Restricted Mass Market Investments": prescribed risk warnings, personalised risk pop-ups, 24-hour cooling-off for first-time investors, ban on referral incentives, strengthened appropriateness tests ([FCA PS22/10](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/ps22-10-strengthening-our-financial-promotion-rules-high-risk-investments-firms-approving-financial-promotions)). Since **7 Feb 2024** an authorised firm needs a specific **s21 approver-gateway permission** to approve promotions for unauthorised persons (gateway opened 6 Nov 2023) ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/2cdf109f/uk-financial-promotions-the-new-gateway-and-other-reforms), [Kroll](https://www.kroll.com/en/insights/publications/financial-compliance-regulation/financial-promotions-gateway)). Net effect: UK compliance overhead is structurally heavier than ECSPR's KIIS regime.

**State fees.** FCA application pricing categories 1–10 run £280 → £225,170 ([FCA fees page](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/authorisation/apply/fees)). Per FEES 3 Annex 1, *operating an electronic system in relation to lending* is **pricing category 5 = £5,640**; advising/arranging intermediary permissions (A.13) are **category 4 = £2,820** ([FCA Handbook FEES 3](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/fees3/fees3s3)).

**Capital.** P2P: higher of **£50,000** or a percentage of loaned funds ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/loan-based-crowdfunding-platforms-summary-our-rules), [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/uk-regulatory-regime-for-crowdfunding-platforms-finalised)). Investment-based platforms: depends on permission set (client-money holding drives it up); materially above the ECSPR floor once operational.

**Setup cost (order of magnitude).** FCA fee £2,820–£5,640 + UK compliance counsel/consultants typically **£40,000–£100,000+** (UNVERIFIED market estimate) + a near-operational platform at application (FCA expects a working or close-to-working website — [JustCoded/FCA guidance summary](https://justcoded.com/blog/fca-authorisation-for-p2p-lending-and-crowdfunding-getting-your-business-ready/)) + wind-down/resolution plan for P2P.

**Timeline.** Statutory: determination within 6 months of a *complete* application (12 months if incomplete); realistic **6–12 months** ([mema consultants, 2026](https://memaconsultants.com/resources/insights/2026-03-04-fca-authorisation-costs-2026)).

**Who it fits.** Founders whose investor base and dealflow are genuinely UK-domestic (the world's deepest equity-crowdfunding retail market — Crowdcube, Republic Europe/ex-Seedrs), with £150k+ of patience and a full-time compliance function. Post-Brexit a UK authorization gives **zero EU access** — Seedrs itself had to get an ECSPR authorization via the Central Bank of Ireland in 2023 to serve the EU (first-party R6 research).

**VERDICT: YES — build /estonia-vs-uk-crowdfunding-license/.** Highest English-language search volume of any jurisdiction, the Brexit angle writes itself ("Crowdcube's parent needed an EU ECSPR licence anyway"), and the cost/friction contrast (£50k capital + RMMI promotions regime + 6–12 months vs EUR 25k-or-insurance + EUR 1,000 fee + ~3–6 months + 27-country passport) is dramatic and fully citable.

## 2. UNITED STATES — SEC + FINRA (Reg CF), mention Reg A+

**License/permission type & regulator.** Regulation Crowdfunding (Reg CF) offerings must run through an SEC-registered intermediary: either a **funding portal** (SEC Form Funding Portal + FINRA funding-portal membership) or a full **broker-dealer** ([SEC Reg CF](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/regulation-crowdfunding), [SEC funding-portal registration guide](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/small-business-compliance-guides/registration-funding-portals)). Issuer cap **$5M per 12 months**; investor limits for non-accredited investors; securities locked up 1 year.

**State fees & capital (funding portal).** FINRA Form FP-NMA application fee **$2,700**; no SEC filing fee ([FINRA funding-portal fees](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/corporate-organization/section-15-funding-portal-member-fees), [LenderKit](https://lenderkit.com/blog/funding-portal-registration-and-costs/)). **No prescribed minimum net capital** — funding portals may not hold customer funds or securities, which is what keeps the prudential bar near zero ([SEC guide](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/small-business-compliance-guides/registration-funding-portals)). FINRA regulates only **62 funding-portal members (active and inactive)** — a strikingly thin field ([FINRA funding portals we regulate](https://www.finra.org/about/entities-we-regulate/funding-portals-we-regulate)).

**Timeline.** FINRA processes a complete FP-NMA within **60 calendar days**; SEC registration effective 30 days after receipt or upon FINRA approval ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/registration-exams-ce/funding-portals/register-new)). Realistic end-to-end **3–6 months** including drafting (UNVERIFIED estimate). Setup cost with US counsel/compliance build-out: **$50,000–$150,000** order of magnitude (UNVERIFIED market estimate; consistent with [LenderKit's](https://lenderkit.com/blog/funding-portal-registration-and-costs/) cost breakdowns).

**Broker-dealer route.** Needed to hold funds/securities, recommend, or run Reg A+/Reg D alongside: FINRA New Member Application, net capital per SEA Rule 15c3-1 ($5k–$250k by activity), FINRA licensed principals, realistic **9–12+ months** and several hundred thousand dollars all-in (UNVERIFIED estimates). **Reg A+** (Tier 2, up to **$75M**/12 months, SEC qualification) is the scale-up path issuers graduate to — worth one paragraph on the page, not a page of its own.

**Who it fits.** Founders targeting US retail money and US issuers. A funding portal is genuinely cheap and fast on paper — the cost sits in FINRA-grade compliance ops, fidelity/E&O, and the fact that a portal cannot touch money (escrow via bank/BD partner). US regime gives zero EU access, and marketing into the EU from a US portal trips ECSPR immediately.

**VERDICT: YES — build /estonia-vs-usa-crowdfunding-license/.** Massive search demand ("how to start a crowdfunding platform / funding portal"), and the comparison is genuinely useful: for a US-only platform Reg CF wins on cost; for anyone whose issuers or investors are European, Estonia wins outright — that honesty makes the page rank and convert. Also the "62 portals for a 330M-person market" stat is excellent copy.

## 3. SWITZERLAND — FINMA (no dedicated regime)

**Position.** Switzerland has **no dedicated crowdfunding license**. FINMA's own fact-sheet approach: each platform must self-assess whether its model triggers banking, fintech, or other authorization ([FINMA fintech page](https://www.finma.ch/en/authorisation/fintech/), [Bär & Karrer, FinTech 2026](https://www.baerkarrer.ch/en/publications/fintech-laws-and-regulations-2026-%E2%80%93-switzerland)). Key mechanics:
- **60-day settlement exemption** (since 1 Aug 2017): funds held purely to settle client transactions, non-interest-bearing and forwarded within 60 days, do not count as public deposits — the lifeline that lets pure-conduit crowdfunding operate license-free ([Legal 500 Switzerland fintech guide](https://www.legal500.com/guides/chapter/switzerland-fintech/)).
- Hold money longer / pool it → **FinTech licence (Banking Act Art. 1b)**: public deposits up to **CHF 100M**, non-invested, non-interest-bearing; minimum capital **CHF 300,000 or 3% of deposits, whichever is higher** ([FINMA](https://www.finma.ch/en/authorisation/fintech/fintech-bewilligung/), [Advapay](https://advapay.eu/emoney-and-payment-institution-licensing/fintech-license-switzerland/)). Full banking licence (CHF 10M capital) if funds are invested/interest-bearing.
- Pure intermediation with payment flows typically still needs AMLA/SRO affiliation; FinSA prospectus/KID duties can bite on the issuer side.

**Cost/timeline.** No license in the conduit model: legal opinion CHF 20k–50k (UNVERIFIED estimate). FinTech licence route: FINMA process realistically **6–12 months**, advisory CHF 100k+ (UNVERIFIED estimates).

**Who it fits.** Swiss-domestic platforms (CHF dealflow, Swiss investors) happy to engineer around the 60-day rule; wealthy, credible market but small and structurally capped — and a Swiss setup confers **zero EU passport** (Switzerland is not in the EEA; ECSPR does not apply).

**VERDICT: YES (moderate priority) — build /estonia-vs-switzerland-crowdfunding-license/.** The absence of a regime IS the story: "Switzerland has no crowdfunding license — here's what you actually need, and why Swiss platforms that want EU investors license in Estonia." Low competition for that exact intent; the CHF 300k-vs-EUR 25k and no-passport-vs-27-states contrasts are clean. Lower volume than UK/US, hence moderate.

## 4. UAE — three separate regimes (DFSA/DIFC, FSRA/ADGM, SCA onshore)

The single most confused jurisdiction in search — three regulators, three rulebooks — which is precisely the SEO opportunity.

**4a. DFSA (DIFC).** Regulated activity *Operating a Crowdfunding Platform* (loan-based and/or investment-based; property variant), Category 4. **Base capital US$140,000** (or higher expenditure-based requirement) ([10 Leaves DIFC crowdfunding guide](https://10leaves.ae/publications/difc/guide-to-the-difc-crowdfunding-business-license), [Equiom](https://www.equiomgroup.com/news/crowdfunding-difc-navigating-dfsa-license-requirements-corporate-governance-consulting)). **Application fee US$5,000; annual fee US$10,000** ([DFSA Fees Module FER](https://dfsaen.thomsonreuters.com/sites/default/files/net_file_store/DFSA1547_10726_VER310125.pdf), [10 Leaves](https://10leaves.ae/publications/difc/guide-to-the-difc-crowdfunding-business-license)). Timeline realistically **4–8 months** incl. DIFC incorporation (UNVERIFIED estimate); add DIFC office + resident SEO/compliance officers — realistic all-in first-year cost **US$150k–300k** (UNVERIFIED estimate).

**4b. ADGM (FSRA).** *Private Financing Platform (PFP)* framework — B2B lending, equity crowdfunding, invoice finance for private enterprises, aimed at professional/institutional money ([ADGM PFP framework](https://www.adgm.com/media/announcements/adgm-launches-new-private-financing-platform), [ADGM PFP guidance](https://www.adgm.com/documents/legal-framework/guidance-and-policy/fsra/guidance-regulatory-framework-for-private-financing-platforms-ver-20231218.pdf)). Prudential Category 4; **base capital US$150,000 where the platform holds client assets** ([ACA on ADGM prudential rules](https://www.acaglobal.com/industry-insights/the-adgms-prudential-rule-changes-for-lower-risk-firms/), [FSRA PRU consultation appendix](https://assets.adgm.com/download/assets/Appendix+1+-+Proposed+amendments+to+PRU.pdf/4a8d610e139511f0be3cce89993ca744)); lower base applies if no client assets are held (Category 4 non-client-asset BCR US$10,000 — UNVERIFIED against the current PRU text; FSRA's 2025 prudential review may move these numbers). Retail reach is restricted vs DFSA.

**4c. SCA (onshore UAE).** Cabinet Resolution No. 36/2022 (amended by No. 139/2023) regulating crowdfunding-platform operators; SCA license mandatory; **paid-up capital AED 1,000,000** (~EUR 250k) ([UAE legislation portal](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1611)).

**Who it fits.** Founders monetizing GCC issuers/investors (real-estate crowdfunding especially) who need local presence and are funded for US$150k+ capital plus DIFC/ADGM operating costs. None of the three confers any EU access.

**VERDICT: YES — build /estonia-vs-dubai-crowdfunding-license/ (frame as "Dubai/UAE", cover all three regimes on one page).** High-intent, high-wealth audience already in Consulting24's crypto funnel (Dubai is a core crypto-license geo for the firm); the three-regulator confusion makes a clear comparison table extremely linkable; capital contrast (US$140–150k / AED 1M vs EUR 25k-or-insurance) is stark. Single page, not three — split later only if it ranks.

## 5. SINGAPORE — MAS

**License/permission type & regulator.** Securities-based crowdfunding requires a **Capital Markets Services (CMS) licence for dealing in capital markets products** under the SFA ([MAS CMS licence page](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/capital-markets/apply-for-licensing-or-registration-of-capital-market-entities/cms-licence)). Lending-based models additionally implicate the Moneylenders Act unless structured as promissory notes/securities.

**Capital.** After MAS's 8 Jun 2016 SCF measures: **base capital S$50,000** (and minimum operational-risk requirement S$50,000, S$100,000 security deposit removed) for SCF operators that do not handle or hold customer monies/assets ([MAS media release](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2016/mas-to-improve-access-to-crowdfunding-for-startups-and-smes)); otherwise the general dealing scale runs **S$250,000 → S$5M** depending on clientele and whether the firm touches client money ([SingaporeLegalAdvice CMS guide](https://singaporelegaladvice.com/law-articles/capital-markets-cms-licence-requirements-singapore/)).

**Fees/timeline/cost.** MAS application fees are modest (four figures); the real bar is qualitative — MAS expects credible shareholders and a management team with financial-services track record, and in practice new-platform CMS applications run **6–12 months** (UNVERIFIED estimate; MAS's own guidance suggests ~4 months for straightforward complete applications). Advisory market rate S$60k–150k (UNVERIFIED estimate).

**Who it fits.** Founders raising for SEA issuers from Singapore-based (largely accredited) investors, with a bankable track record — MAS effectively filters out first-time founders. Incumbents: Funding Societies, Fundnel. No EU access.

**VERDICT: YES (moderate priority) — build /estonia-vs-singapore-crowdfunding-license/.** Singapore is the APAC search anchor and pairs naturally with Consulting24's existing vs-page pattern. The honest pitch — "S$50k capital looks cheap, but MAS admission standards and timelines are the real cost; Estonia is the faster, retail-capable, 27-country alternative" — is defensible and differentiated. Moderate volume, so build after UK/US/UAE.

## 6. CANADA — CSA provinces + NI 45-110

**Regime.** **NI 45-110 Start-up Crowdfunding Registration and Prospectus Exemptions**, harmonized nationally 21 Sep 2021 ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/a55bf494/canadian-securities-regulators-adopt-national-instrument-45-110-start-up-crowdfunding), [CSA](https://www.securities-administrators.ca/news/canadian-securities-regulators-adopt-new-nationally-harmonized-start-up-crowdfunding-rules/)). Issuer cap **CA$1.5M per 12 months**; investor cap **CA$2,500** per distribution (CA$10,000 with registered-dealer suitability advice). Ontario also retains the separate 45-108 crowdfunding regime.

**Portal routes.** (a) Register as a dealer — typically **exempt market dealer** under NI 31-103: **minimum capital CA$50,000**, insurance (lesser of $50k/person or $200k+), UDP + CCO, NRD filings ([Lexology/NI 31-103 summaries](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=676475ec-4e99-4f91-a66a-4142dd24868f), [Wright Business Law](https://wrightbusinesslaw.ca/how-to-set-up-an-exempt-market-dealer-in-ontario/)); or (b) rely on the **registration exemption for start-up crowdfunding portals** — no dealer registration, but no advice permitted, prescribed forms, and semi-annual certification of 6-month financial resources ([NRF](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/a55bf494/canadian-securities-regulators-adopt-national-instrument-45-110-start-up-crowdfunding)). EMD registration realistically **3–6 months**, advisory CA$40k–100k (UNVERIFIED estimates).

**Who it fits.** Canada-domestic community/startup portals content with a CA$1.5M issuer cap (vs ECSPR's EUR 5M) or full EMDs doing exempt-market distribution. Provincial passporting exists but the economics are thin; no EU access.

**VERDICT: NO standalone vs-page.** Search volume for "crowdfunding license Canada" is modest, the regime's CA$1.5M cap makes the audience micro-platforms with no EUR 37k budget, and Canadian founders wanting Europe are rare. Cover Canada in a global hub/roundup page ("crowdfunding platform license requirements by country") and internally link it to the Estonia money page.

## 7. AUSTRALIA — ASIC (CSF intermediary)

**Regime.** Crowd-sourced funding under Corporations Act Part 6D.3A: a **CSF intermediary must hold an AFS licence expressly authorizing the crowd-funding service**, applied for via the ASIC Regulatory Portal per RG 262 ([ASIC CSF intermediary page](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/financial-services/crowd-sourced-funding/applying-to-be-a-crowd-sourced-funding-intermediary/), [RG 262](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/find-a-document/regulatory-guides/rg-262-crowd-sourced-funding-guide-for-intermediaries/)). Issuers: eligible unlisted public companies under **A$25M** assets and revenue, raising up to **A$5M**/12 months; retail investors capped at **A$10,000** per company per 12 months ([ASIC CSF](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/financial-services/crowd-sourced-funding/), [Hall & Wilcox](https://hallandwilcox.com.au/news/crowd-sourced-funding-why-you-might-or-might-not/)). Intermediaries carry explicit statutory **gatekeeper obligations** (issuer checks before publishing offers).

**Fees/capital/timeline.** AFS application fee for a body corporate **A$1,485** (online, 2025; indexed annually) ([Lawpath](https://lawpath.com.au/blog/how-do-i-apply-for-an-australian-financial-services-licence), [ASIC fees](https://www.asic.gov.au/for-business-and-companies/forms-and-fees/all-fees/)). No large fixed base capital: RG 166 tailored financial requirements (positive net assets, solvency, cash-flow projections; surplus-liquid-funds add-ons where client money is held — exact triggers UNVERIFIED here). ASIC licensed its first 7 CSF intermediaries in Jan 2018, ~3.5 months after applications opened ([ASIC 18-004MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2018-releases/18-004mr-asic-licenses-first-crowd-sourced-funding-intermediaries/)); realistic today **6–12 months** for a new fintech AFSL (UNVERIFIED estimate). Advisory A$50k–100k (UNVERIFIED estimate).

**Who it fits.** Australia-domestic equity platforms (Birchal dominates). Purely domestic regime; no EU relevance and little founder overlap with an Estonian decision.

**VERDICT: NO standalone vs-page.** Decent domestic regime but almost zero commercial overlap: Australian founders build for Australia; queries comparing Australia and EU licensing are negligible. Include in the global hub page only.

## 8. SAUDI ARABIA — CMA + SAMA (brief)

Dual regime: **equity/securities crowdfunding under the CMA** (capital-market institution license; CMA also allows institutions licensed for *arranging* to run debt-instrument crowdfunding platforms — [CMA announcement](https://cma.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/NEWS/Pages/CMA_N_3862.aspx); early platforms came through the CMA FinTech Lab / Experimental Permit); **debt-based crowdfunding (crowdlending) under SAMA** — Rules for Engaging in Debt-Based Crowdfunding (2021, updated): SAMA license mandatory, **minimum capital SAR 5,000,000** (~EUR 1.2M), 5-year renewable license ([SAMA rules PDF](https://www.sama.gov.sa/en-US/Laws/FinanceRules/Rules_for_Engaging_in_Debt_Based_Crowdfunding-EN.pdf), [SAMA rulebook](https://rulebook.sama.gov.sa/en/rules-engaging-debt-based-crowdfunding)). Local incorporation, Saudization and Arabic-market operations effectively require a domestic partner.

**VERDICT: NO page.** SAR 5M capital, local-presence reality and an Arabic-first market mean no realistic funnel overlap with an Estonian EU license; one row in the hub table suffices.

## 9. ISRAEL — ISA (brief)

**Offering Coordinator** regime under the Securities Regulations (Offer of Securities through an Offering Coordinator), 2017 (in force 2018): ISA registration, proof of technical capability, insurance, **ILS 100,000 deposit**; issuer caps **ILS 4M** per 12 months (up to **ILS 6M** with a leading investor/audited statements) ([Herzog](https://herzoglaw.co.il/en/news-and-insights/crowdfunding-regulations-notice-to-companies-that-wish-to-register-as-an-offer-coordinator/), [Howard Kennedy comparative review](https://www.howardkennedy.com/latest/blog/regulation-of-crowdfunding-in-the-uk-us-and-israel_a-comparative-review)).

**VERDICT: NO page.** Tiny caps (~EUR 1–1.5M), Hebrew-language domestic market, minimal search demand in English. Hub-table row only; the interesting Israeli angle (Israeli startups raising from EU retail via an Estonian ECSP) belongs as a paragraph on the Estonia money page, not a vs-page.
