## 1. Regulator: Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian FSA)

The single competent authority for ECSPR authorisation and supervision in Estonia is **Finantsinspektsioon** (Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority). It accepts applications, runs the completeness and substantive review, grants/withdraws the licence, receives ongoing reports, and handles Art 18 passport notifications. Since **18 March 2026** applications must be filed through the Finantsinspektsioon application portal (taotlus.fi.ee) and **in Estonian** (https://www.fi.ee/en/investment/applying-operating-licence/operating-licence-crowdfunding).

Not the FIU: the Financial Intelligence Unit is Estonia's AML authority (former crypto-VASP licensor) and plays no part in crowdfunding authorisation. See the correction block above.

## 2. Legal basis

- **Regulation (EU) 2020/1503** — the European Crowdfunding Service Providers Regulation (ECSPR), in application since 10 November 2021. Directly applicable in Estonia; one licence covers lending-based and investment-based (equity/securities) crowdfunding for business projects up to EUR 5M per project owner per 12 months. EUR-Lex: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32020R1503
- **Directive (EU) 2020/1504** — carve-out of CSPs from MiFID II.
- **Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/2112** — RTS with the authorisation application form and information standards (the FI application follows its annex): https://www.fi.ee/en/investment/applying-operating-licence/operating-licence-crowdfunding
- **Delegated Regulations (EU) 2022/2114** (entry knowledge test / simulation of ability to bear loss) and **2022/2119** (KIIS technical standards) — EUR-Lex summary: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=LEGISSUM:4486505
- **Estonian implementation:** the Riigikogu implemented ECSPR through the Act amending the Financial Supervision Authority Act (Finantsinspektsiooni seadus) and related acts (draft **422 SE**), designating Finantsinspektsioon as competent authority; FI began accepting applications on 10.11.2021 (https://www.fi.ee/en/news/crowdfunding-come-under-supervision-finantsinspektsioon; https://m.riigikogu.ee/pressiteated/riigikogu-vottis-vastu-kaheksa-seadust-ja-kaks-otsust/). National overlays that apply to a live platform: Advertising Act (Reklaamiseadus), Consumer Protection Act, Language Act.

## 3. State fee and other regulator costs

- **Application processing fee: EUR 1,000**, payable to Finantsinspektsioon with the licence application (fi.ee licensing page). There is **no separate riigilõiv** beyond this menetlustasu for the ECSPR licence.
- **Cross-border (Art 18) notification: EUR 0** — "no fee for cross-border notification" (fi.ee licensing page).
- **Ongoing supervision fee (järelevalvetasu):** for CSPs the volume-based component is **0.001–0.1% of amounts intermediated** in the prior year, per the Financial Supervision Authority Act (https://www.riigiteataja.ee/akt/FIS).

## 4. Own funds — EUR 25,000 or insurance (ECSPR Art 11)

Prudential safeguards must at all times equal at least **the higher of (a) EUR 25,000 and (b) one quarter of the fixed overheads of the preceding year**, reviewed annually (and including, where loans are facilitated, 3 months of loan-servicing costs). ECSPR Art 11(1), EUR-Lex CELEX:32020R1503.

**Exact form of the alternative (Art 11(2)):** the safeguard may take the form of
(a) **own funds** (CET1 items per CRR), or
(b) **"an insurance policy covering the territories of the Union where crowdfunding offers are actively marketed, or a comparable guarantee"**, or
(c) **a combination of (a) and (b)**.

The insurance policy must (Art 11(6)-(7)): have an initial term of no less than one year with ≥90 days' cancellation notice, be taken from an EU-authorised (or equivalently supervised) insurer, be provided by a third party, and cover at minimum the risk of: loss of documents; misrepresentations or misleading statements; acts, errors or omissions resulting in breach of legal/regulatory obligations or of the duty of skill and care toward clients; failure to establish, implement and maintain appropriate conflict-of-interest procedures; and losses from business interruption, system failures or process management failures.

**Marketing note:** the docx phrase "share capital of EUR 25,000" is a simplification — the requirement is *own funds*, not nominal share capital (in practice EUR 25,000 of paid-in capital satisfies it at launch). The insurance route at "approximately EUR 1,000–1,500/month" is X24Consulting's first-party market pricing (UNVERIFIED against any insurer quote — present as "from approx. EUR 1,000/month, subject to insurer underwriting").

## 5. What the application file must contain (ECSPR Art 12 + DR 2022/2112, plain English)

Finantsinspektsioon's checklist tracks Art 12(2) — 18 information categories (fi.ee licensing page; EUR-Lex Art 12):

1. Company name, legal form (OÜ works), articles of association, address, website.
2. **Programme of operations** — which crowdfunding services (facilitating loans and/or placing of transferable securities/admitted instruments), on which platform.
3. **Governance arrangements and internal controls** — compliance, risk management, accounting procedures.
4. **ICT description** — systems, resources and procedures for data processing and security.
5. **Operational risk assessment** and the prudential-safeguard calculation (Art 11 evidence: own funds and/or insurance policy).
6. **Business continuity plan** — critically, how loans keep being serviced and investor data stays accessible if the platform fails.
7. **Identity of management board members and shareholders with ≥20%** — plus proof of good repute (no criminal record in commercial/insolvency/financial-services/AML/fraud law) and proof of sufficient knowledge, skills and experience (CVs). This is where the mandatory CEO/CFO and the compliance + risk officer CVs from the offer live.
8. **Conflict-of-interest rules** — including the Art 8 ban on the platform having any participation in offers on its own platform and the related-party restrictions (owners with ≥20% cannot be project owners on the platform).
9. **Outsourcing arrangements** (IT may be outsourced — matches the offer's "IT specialist may be outsourced").
10. **Complaint-handling procedures** (with the DR 2022/2117 template regime).
11. **Payment services setup** — whether payments are handled by the CSP itself (needs PSD2 authorisation) or via a licensed PSP partner (Art 10(5) route — the standard model).
12. **KIIS verification procedures** — how the platform checks the key investment information sheet is complete, correct and clear.
13. **Investment-limit and investor-protection procedures** for non-sophisticated investors (entry test, loss simulation, EUR 1,000/5% threshold flow, reflection period).

Statutory clock: FI must tell the applicant **within 25 working days** whether the file is complete; a decision (grant or refusal) follows **within 3 months of a complete application**, notified within 3 working days (fi.ee; Art 12(4),(8)).

## 7. EU passporting (ECSPR Art 18): 27 countries, EUR 0, 15 days

One Estonian licence covers all crowdfunding services EU-wide after a simple notification:

- The CSP submits to Finantsinspektsioon a list of the member states where it intends to provide services, the identity of the persons responsible, and the intended start date (Art 18(1)).
- The home NCA communicates this to the host NCAs and ESMA (Art 18 — communication within 10 working days of receipt, UNVERIFIED exact sub-paragraph wording in this pass).
- The provider **may start providing services in the host state from the date of receipt of that communication, or at the latest 15 calendar days after submitting the notification** (fi.ee licensing page: services "may commence from notification receipt or within 15 calendar days maximum").
- **Cost: EUR 0** — Finantsinspektsioon charges no fee for the cross-border notification (fi.ee licensing page).

This is the cheapest pan-EU distribution mechanism in regulated finance — compare a MiFID passport or 27 national consumer-credit regimes. ESMA data shows ~78% of amounts invested through Estonian-authorised providers already come from abroad — the strongest statistic for an "Estonian seat, EU market" strategy (first-party research from ESMA country data; UNVERIFIED this pass).

## 9. Ongoing obligations after the licence

- **Annual regulator reporting (Art 16):** each year, a confidential list of all funded projects — project owner, amount raised, instrument, and investor breakdown by sophistication and residence (EUR-Lex CELEX:32020R1503, Art 16, verified quote: the CSP "shall annually and on a confidential basis provide a list of projects funded through its crowdfunding platform to the competent authority").
- **Key Investment Information Sheet (Art 23):** drafted by the *project owner* for every offer, maximum **6 sides of A4**, in the language(s) of the marketed member states; the platform must have procedures to verify completeness, correctness and clarity and to flag/suspend deficient offers. Technical standards: DR (EU) 2022/2119.
- **Non-sophisticated investor protections (Arts 21–22):** entry knowledge test + simulation of ability to bear loss (DR (EU) 2022/2114); before any investment above **the higher of EUR 1,000 or 5% of the investor's net worth**, the platform must give a specific risk warning and obtain explicit consent plus proof of understanding (Art 21(7)); plus a **four-calendar-day pre-contractual reflection period** during which a non-sophisticated investor can revoke the investment offer without penalty (EUR-Lex summary: providers must "provide a 4-day reflection period"; https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=LEGISSUM:4486505).
- **Prudential maintenance (Art 11):** own funds/insurance level recalculated annually against fixed overheads.
- **Bulletin board discipline (Art 25):** a platform may run a board where investors advertise interest in buying/selling — but with no internal matching engine and no binding price formation, otherwise it becomes a MiFID trading venue.
- **Conflict-of-interest, outsourcing, complaint and marketing rules** (Arts 8, 9, 7, 27) plus Estonian Advertising Act / Consumer Protection Act / Language Act overlays for Estonian-market marketing.
- **Supervision fee** to Finantsinspektsioon: volume component 0.001–0.1% of intermediated amounts (Financial Supervision Authority Act, riigiteataja.ee/akt/FIS).

## 10. Company-level facts (Estonia)

- **Vehicle:** an Estonian OÜ (private limited company) is sufficient; registration takes **1–5 business days** (offer docx; e-Business Register standard is same-day to a few days).
- **Substance:** Finantsinspektsioon expects the licensed entity to be genuinely run from Estonia — physical office, management genuinely directing the business from Estonia, no nominee directors; a locally responsible compliance function is assessed in fit-and-proper review (market practice for FI-supervised entities; e.g. https://key2law.com/en/news/using-estonias-e-residency-to-form-companies-legal-limits-and-hidden-compliance-costs). The Consulting24 offer's mandatory **salaried CEO and CFO** plus **compliance-officer and risk-officer CVs** (active employment not mandatory for the latter two) match this expectation; **IT may be outsourced** (offer docx).
- **e-Residency:** works for founding, signing and administering the OÜ remotely (https://learn.e-resident.gov.ee/hc/en-gb/articles/360000633817-Business-licenses) — but it does not substitute for the local substance FI requires of a licensee. Founders can be 100% foreign; the operating team cannot be 100% remote.
- **Taxation (verified for 2026):** **0% corporate income tax on retained and reinvested profits**; distributions taxed at **22/78** (~22% of the gross distribution). The planned 2026 increase to 24% was **cancelled by the Riigikogu in December 2025**, and the temporary 2% defence/security tax on corporate profits was **abolished in June 2025 before taking effect** (EY: https://www.ey.com/en_ee/insights/tax/significant-tax-changes-in-estonia-in-2025-2026; PwC: https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/estonia/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income). VAT is 24%; the Consulting24 fee itself is quoted "no VAT added" (offer docx).
- **Payments:** the platform itself should not touch client money without a PSD2 licence — the standard structure is a partnership with a licensed PSP/EMI declared in the application (Art 10(5)). Banking/EMI onboarding is a separate third-party compliance process (offer docx, Important Notes).

## FAQ (ready to adapt for the page)

**1. Who issues the crowdfunding license in Estonia?**
Finantsinspektsioon, the Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority (Estonian FSA). It is the single competent authority under Regulation (EU) 2020/1503 (ECSPR). The Estonian FIU has no role in crowdfunding authorisation — it is the AML authority. Applications go through the Finantsinspektsioon portal (mandatory since 18 March 2026) and must be in Estonian.

**2. How much does an Estonian crowdfunding license cost?**
The regulator's processing fee is EUR 1,000. Own funds of EUR 25,000 (or an equivalent insurance policy) are required. Consulting24's full application and documentation service is EUR 37,000, with an optional EUR 3,000 business plan. Ongoing supervision fees are volume-based (0.001–0.1% of intermediated amounts).

**3. How long does it take to get a crowdfunding license in Estonia?**
Statutory clocks: Finantsinspektsioon confirms completeness within 25 working days and must decide within 3 months of a complete application. In practice, plan ~6–9 months from filing including Q&A rounds — and ~6–12 months from company formation to licence. Consulting24's pre-reviewed documentation package can shorten preparation by roughly 3 months.

**4. What is the minimum capital for an ECSPR crowdfunding license?**
The prudential requirement is the higher of EUR 25,000 or one quarter of the previous year's fixed overheads (ECSPR Art 11). It can be met with own funds, with "an insurance policy covering the territories of the Union where crowdfunding offers are actively marketed", or a combination of both. Insurance replacing own funds is available from roughly EUR 1,000–1,500/month (insurer-dependent).

**5. What does one Estonian license let me do across the EU?**
Everything ECSPR covers — facilitating business loans and placing transferable securities/admitted instruments up to EUR 5,000,000 per project owner per 12 months — in all EU member states. Cross-border expansion is a free Art 18 notification through Finantsinspektsioon; you can start serving a new country at the latest 15 calendar days after notifying.

**6. What must the application contain?**
The Art 12 file per Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/2112: business plan/programme of operations, governance and internal controls, ICT and data-security description, operational risk assessment, prudential-safeguard proof, business continuity plan, management CVs and clean-record evidence, conflict-of-interest rules, outsourcing arrangements, complaint handling, the payment-services setup, KIIS verification procedures, and non-sophisticated-investor protection procedures — roughly 18 documentation categories.

**7. What team do I need in Estonia?**
A salaried CEO and CFO, plus a compliance officer and a risk officer (CVs assessed in fit-and-proper review; active employment of the latter two is not mandatory at application). IT can be outsourced. Finantsinspektsioon expects the company to be genuinely managed from Estonia — an empty shell run entirely from abroad will not pass.

**8. Can I use e-Residency to get an Estonian crowdfunding license?**
e-Residency lets founders establish and administer the Estonian OÜ fully remotely — company registration takes 1–5 business days. But the licensed platform itself needs real Estonian substance: local office, local management activity, and named responsible persons. Foreign ownership is 100% fine.

**9. Who currently holds an Estonian crowdfunding license?**
As of August 2026, exactly one company: Estateguru OÜ (lending-based real-estate crowdfunding, licensed 8 May 2023). Crowdestate AS handed back its licence in April 2026. No investment-based (equity) crowdfunding platform has ever been licensed in Estonia — the equity slot is empty, so a new applicant would be the country's first.

**10. What taxes will my Estonian crowdfunding company pay?**
Estonia charges 0% corporate income tax on retained and reinvested profits; tax (22/78, ~22%) applies only when profits are distributed. The planned 2026 rate increase was cancelled in December 2025, and the 2% corporate defence tax was abolished in June 2025 before it ever applied. This makes Estonia one of the most reinvestment-friendly EU bases for a scaling platform.

**11. What are the ongoing obligations after licensing?**
Annual confidential reporting of funded projects to Finantsinspektsioon; verifying a Key Investment Information Sheet (max 6 A4 sides, drafted by the project owner) for every offer; running the entry knowledge test and loss-bearing simulation for retail investors; extra warnings and consent for investments above the higher of EUR 1,000 or 5% of the investor's net worth; honouring the 4-calendar-day reflection period; maintaining Art 11 own funds/insurance; and paying an annual volume-based supervision fee.

**12. Is there an investor limit or offer limit under ECSPR?**
Per project owner, offers are capped at EUR 5,000,000 over any 12-month period — above that you are in prospectus/MiFID territory. There is no cap for sophisticated investors; non-sophisticated investors trigger the warning-and-consent flow above the EUR 1,000 / 5%-of-net-worth threshold and always get the 4-day reflection period.

**13. Can my platform run a secondary market?**
Not a true exchange. ECSPR Art 25 allows a "bulletin board" where investors advertise buying/selling interest in past deals — but without an internal matching engine or binding price formation. Anything more becomes a MiFID trading venue requiring an investment-firm licence.

**14. Lending-based vs investment-based — does the license differ?**
One ECSPR authorisation covers both service types; you apply for the services you intend to provide in the programme of operations. In Estonia, the only current licensee (Estateguru) does lending only. Both Estonian licences ever issued went to lending platforms — equity/investment-based crowdfunding is a first-mover opportunity.
