Date: 2026-08-01. Scope: Estonian ECSPR (Regulation (EU) 2020/1503) crowdfunding service provider authorization, delivered directly by Consulting24 / X24Consulting OÜ (reg. 16971898, Tallinn). Prices from the live commercial offer (crowdfunding-docx): EUR 37,000 full application + documentation; EUR 3,000 optional business plan; staged EUR 3,000 + EUR 34,000.

## 0. REGULATOR VERIFICATION — the FIU/Finantsinspektsioon discrepancy (RESOLVED)

**The offer docx is wrong on the regulator and must be corrected before anything goes live.**

- The docx says the licensing process runs with the "Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU)" and calls the base documents "FIU-approved crowdfunding license documentation."
- **Verified correct regulator: Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision Authority).** Its official page "The operating licence for crowdfunding" states: the licence application fee is **1,000 euros**, completeness is checked **within 25 working days**, the decision is made **within three months of receipt of the complete application**, applications **must be submitted in Estonian**, and **from 18.03.2026 only via the Finantsinspektsioon application portal** — https://www.fi.ee/en/investment/applying-operating-licence/operating-licence-crowdfunding
- The FIU (Rahapesu Andmebüroo) is Estonia's AML authority; it issued the *legacy virtual-currency licenses* (pre-MiCA). It has **no role in issuing ECSPR authorizations**. Estonia's two ECSPR licences ever issued (Crowdestate 06.03.2023, Estateguru 08.05.2023) were both granted by Finantsinspektsioon (see e.g. https://www.fi.ee/en/news/estateguru-was-granted-operating-authorisation-provider-crowdfunding-services).
- **Action:** every public page, the offer docx, and contracts use **only "Finantsinspektsioon"** (first mention: "Finantsinspektsioon, the Estonian Financial Supervision Authority"). The "two rounds of FIU review" claim becomes "two completed rounds of Finantsinspektsioon review" (see §1 for exact permitted wording). Using "FIU" on a crowdfunding page would also cannibalize/confuse the crypto-side content where FIU means the old VASP register — an SEO and credibility own-goal.

## 1. Positioning statement + differentiators

### Positioning statement

> **Consulting24 gets your Estonian crowdfunding licence (EU ECSPR) filed right the first time — in Estonian, by an Estonian firm, using a complete application package already stress-tested through two rounds of Finantsinspektsioon review. EUR 37,000 fixed fee, published up front. One licence, 27+ EEA countries via free Article 18 passporting. No approval guarantees — no consultancy can honestly offer one — but a documentation baseline that has already survived the regulator's questions.**

Short form (hero): *"Estonia crowdfunding license (ECSPR) — EUR 37,000 fixed. Direct delivery in Tallinn, application filed in Estonian, EU-wide passport."*

### Differentiators (all TRUE and provable — with exact permitted wording)

1. **Direct delivery by an Estonian company, in Estonian.** Finantsinspektsioon requires the application in Estonian, via its portal (mandatory since 18.03.2026) — https://www.fi.ee/en/investment/applying-operating-licence/operating-licence-crowdfunding. X24Consulting OÜ is an Estonian company (reg. 16971898, verifiable at ariregister.rik.ee) with an Estonian founder-CEO. Most foreign "EU crowdfunding license" consultancies must subcontract the Estonian-language filing; Consulting24 does not. *Claim: "We prepare and file in Estonian — the only language Finantsinspektsioon accepts."*

2. **A complete Estonian ECSPR application package already exists in-house.** 30+ documents mapped to the 18 information categories of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/2112 — AML internal rules, investor T&Cs, project-owner agreement, KIIS assessment procedure, knowledge-test question bank, ICT/business-continuity/outsourcing policies, management fit-and-proper files, complaint handling, payment-arrangement confirmations, and the Article 18 cross-border notification template. *Claim: "You are not paying us to write documents from scratch — the full Estonian-language package exists and is adapted to your business model." Provable: show the document index (not contents) in sales calls.*

3. **Documentation refined through two completed rounds of Finantsinspektsioon review.** Exact permitted wording: *"Our base documentation has been through two full rounds of Finantsinspektsioon's review process in a live application, and was updated to address the regulator's comments."* What may NOT be said: "FIU-approved," "regulator-approved," "pre-approved," "passed regulator review" as an implication of endorsement or guaranteed outcome. The docx's own framing — customization *"may potentially"* reduce preparation timeline by ~3 months — is the ceiling: present the ~3-month saving as a possibility on the *documentation-preparation* phase only, never on Finantsinspektsioon's review clock.

4. **Track record you can check: 500+ licenses across 15+ jurisdictions in 8 years; founder Mardo Soo has personally set up 200+ crypto/fintech/financial-services entities.** (Existing house claims from consulting24.co / llms.txt.) The crowdfunding line reuses this machinery — same jurisdiction-selection honesty, same "we tell you if Estonia is wrong for you" stance the crypto pages take.

5. **Published fixed pricing — near-unique in this market.** Of the competitor set surveyed (§2), only two Baltic budget shops publish any number, and every law-firm/Western-European provider is "contact us." A public "EUR 37,000 fixed, staged 3k + 34k, EUR 300/h beyond scope, third-party costs listed separately" box is itself a differentiator, mirroring the crypto side's "€6,000 fixed fee, all-in. No hidden fees" pattern.

**Explicit anti-differentiator to state on the page (builds trust, mirrors docx §8):** Consulting24 does **not** guarantee licence approval or regulator response times, and banking/EMI onboarding is subject to independent third-party compliance review.

### Market-context facts safe to use (each date-stamped and sourced)

- Estonia currently has **one** authorised crowdfunding service provider (Estateguru OÜ, lending) after Crowdestate's licence was withdrawn at its own request on 13.04.2026 — cite Finantsinspektsioon's register/news, fi.ee (first-party research verified against live registers, Aug 2026). Frame as opportunity: "the field is open," not "you'll be the only one" (someone may be in the pipeline — unknowable).
- **181 authorised CSPs across 21 EU member states** (ESMA market report on 2024 data, published 14.01.2026) — https://www.esma.europa.eu (crowdfunding/ECSPR section). Estonia has one of the highest cross-border investment shares in the EU (~78% of amounts invested via Estonian providers come from abroad, same ESMA report) — the single best statistic for "license in Estonia, sell everywhere."
- Article 18 passporting: a free notification, 15-calendar-day clock, no host-state fee — Regulation (EU) 2020/1503 Art 18, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2020/1503/oj.
- Own funds: the higher of EUR 25,000 or 1/4 of prior-year fixed overheads, **or** an equivalent insurance policy/guarantee, or a combination — ECSPR Art 11. The docx's "~EUR 1,000–1,500/month" insurance cost is Consulting24's own market estimate — label it "indicative, from recent client quotes" (UNVERIFIED publicly).

### Verdict: where EUR 37,000 sits

- **Above** the Baltic budget packagers (EUR 14,600–20,000 "from" prices). Do not pretend to be the cheapest; two competitors visibly undercut on sticker price.
- **Inside** the realistic total-cost band platforms themselves report (EuroCrowd: 70% expected EUR 30k+; 40% expected 50k+).
- **Far below** the Netherlands/Luxembourg/Western-law-firm tracks (EUR 100k+ total; EUR 30k in state fees alone in LU).

### How to present it (the argument, in page copy order)

1. **Lead with fixed and published, not cheap.** "EUR 37,000, fixed. Published because we don't do 'contact us for pricing.'" The "from EUR 15–20k" competitors are unanchored minimums for document drafting; Consulting24's number is the whole engagement.
2. **Reframe against total cost of ownership:** the Estonian route's third-party costs are tiny and enumerable — EUR 1,000 state fee, EUR 25,000 own funds (which is the client's capital, not a cost — or replaceable with insurance at ~EUR 1,000–1,500/month indicative), company formation. Contrast with NL (EUR 100k+) and LU (EUR 30k state fee).
3. **Justify the premium with the three provables:** existing full Estonian package + two Finantsinspektsioon review rounds already absorbed + direct Estonian-language filing. The buyer is paying to skip the expensive first-draft/first-rejection loop that "from EUR 15k" doesn't price in.
4. **Stage it exactly as the docx does:** EUR 3,000 at company registration + onboarding, EUR 34,000 at application/documentation phase. Staging reads as risk-sharing.

**EUR 37,000 — Full Estonian crowdfunding licence application (fixed)**
- Estonian company registration coordination (1–5 business days) and onboarding (1–2 weeks)

**Not included (listed transparently, house style):** EUR 1,000 Finantsinspektsioon application fee; EUR 25,000 own funds *or* insurance replacing own funds (~EUR 1,000–1,500/month, indicative); state/notary fees; CEO/CFO salaries; ongoing supervision costs.

## 4. DO-NOT-CLAIM list (compliance-gated phrasing)

| Never say | Say instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "FIU", "Financial Intelligence Unit", "FIU-approved" (in any crowdfunding context) | "Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision Authority)" | Wrong authority; verified at fi.ee (§0). Also collides with legacy crypto-FIU content. The offer docx itself must be corrected. |
| "Guaranteed approval", "100% success rate", "we get you licensed" | "We prepare the application to Finantsinspektsioon's requirements; approval and response times rest solely with the regulator" | Docx §8 already disclaims this; guarantee language invites liability + regulator ire. |
| "Regulator-approved documentation / advisor / partner" | "Base documentation refined through two completed rounds of Finantsinspektsioon review" | Finantsinspektsioon approves licences, not consultancies or template packs. |
| "Licence in 3 months" / "fast-track approval" | "Statutory decision within 3 months of a complete application; realistically ~3–6 months of review, and timelines can extend" | 25-working-day completeness check + Q&A rounds; "fast-track" implies a relationship that doesn't exist. |
| "Saves you 3 months" (as fact) | "May shorten the documentation-preparation phase by up to ~3 months" | Docx says "may potentially"; the saving applies to drafting, never the regulator's clock. |
| "Estonia's only/first [anything]" or "you'll be the only licensed platform" | "As of [date], Finantsinspektsioon's register lists one authorised provider [link]" | Unverifiable superlatives are the classic misleading-marketing finding (platform-v2 critique explicitly kills "only in Europe" claims); registers change. |
| "No capital required" | "EUR 25,000 own funds — or an insurance policy replacing own funds (ECSPR Art 11)" | Art 11 floor is real; the insurance route is the honest softener. |
| "One licence, zero paperwork EU-wide" | "EU/EEA passporting via a free Article 18 notification (15-calendar-day clock) per country" | Passporting is cheap but not automatic. |
| Bank-account or EMI onboarding promises | "Banking/EMI onboarding is subject to independent third-party compliance review; we make introductions" | Docx §8; house crypto pages use the same hedge. |
| Any Osalus/platform-side claims: "your shares survive platform insolvency", "direct Business Register title", "invest in 3 minutes" | (omit entirely from consultancy pages) | Gated by platform-v2 critique (14-critique-next-actions.md): title-regime unproven pending Estonian counsel; not this service's claim to make. |
