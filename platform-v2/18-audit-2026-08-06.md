# Osalus — Consolidated Action Plan (2026-08-06)

## 1. VERDICT

**No. The product and the legal package describe two different companies.** The 2026-08-02 nominee reversal was applied to the marketing surface and to lisa-05 §6.3, and stopped there. The two documents the investor actually signs and relies on — T2 (terms of use) and T4 (mandatory website information) — contain zero occurrences of "esindaja", "nominee" or "AureviaFund", and T2 §8 still promises the investor direct entry on the shareholder list and survival of the platform's collapse. That promise is false under the adopted model and it sits behind the app's terms-acceptance gate. On top of that, an unauthorised firm is publishing three named companies with OPEN status LEDs, live commitment meters and a "Buy now" button, and a fee page headed "Fees, in full" that omits EUR 1 500 of the issuer's lifetime cost. This is not a polish problem: shipped as-is it hands Finantsinspektsioon a ready-made art 27 misleading-marketing file before the application is decided.

The HTML builds are the cleaner half of the package — the nominee is disclosed on offer cards, the process ledger, the portfolio, the footer and the bulletin board, and the art 27 discipline mostly holds (no projections, no countdowns, no return figures, and the app even models an art 27 enforcement queue). Two internal contradictions should be fixed before any external read: the dashboard prints a EUR portfolio valuation on the same product whose portfolio page says no valuations are shown, and the signup flow promises "we will warn you, not block you" when lisa-19 §3.5 and §4.4 both mandate hard blocks. Fix the six blockers and items 7-12 before the package goes to Finantsinspektsioon; the rest can follow in the same pass.

I verified findings by running the KYC service on an alternate port and driving the deployed app in a live browser rather than reasoning from the source alone, and the results are worse than a read-through suggests.

The KYC service can be killed by any unauthenticated stranger with one request — I did it twice, two different ways: `GET /%` throws URIError inside the un-awaited async handler, and a 2 MB POST to /api/kyc/webhook rejects readBody with no try/catch. Both produce an unhandled rejection and Node exits. DEPLOY.md's tunnel recipe therefore puts you in a bind — the documented python3 static server cannot serve /api at all (KYC is simply dead in the shared link), while tunnelling the Node server publishes all of it. None of this is reachable today in one specific sense: DIDIT_WORKFLOW_ID and DIDIT_WEBHOOK_SECRET are both empty in .env, so the whole integration currently fails closed. That is the only reason the KYC path is not already a live incident.

On the app, the single worst defect is that the first screen after sign-in is a dead end. nav() only sets location.hash and relies on the hashchange listener to re-render, so when the target equals the current hash nothing happens — and acceptTerms() navigates to exactly the hash the user is already on. I reproduced it: terms accepted, state written, terms gate still on screen, dashboard never appears. Every new user hits this and the Continue button looks broken. The same root cause silently desyncs the account-type switcher, where I confirmed state flips to corporate while the UI keeps claiming private — on a control that governs onboarding requirements.

Responsive is not a matter of polish here: below 781px both the icon rail and the side nav are display:none with nothing replacing them, and I measured zero visible nav links at 375px. On a phone and on a 768px tablet the user can reach the dashboard and nothing else. The topbar overflows to 718px, scrolling the whole document sideways, and the mandatory ECSPR risk bar grows to 142px against 74px of body padding, covering page content while toasts render on top of the warning itself.

Two findings deserve attention beyond their engineering cost because a supervisor will read them as compliance defects rather than bugs. The art 22 one-click withdrawal is implemented as `javascript:withdraw('laine')` — it will stop working, silently and invisibly, the moment a CSP lands on the deploy bundle. And on the consent screen stamped "YOUR ACCEPTANCE IS RECORDED WITH A TIMESTAMP", both document links sit inside the label that drives the checkbox, so clicking through to read the terms toggles your acceptance.

Two things are genuinely well built and I want to be clear that I checked rather than assumed. Credential handling is right: the API key never leaves the server, /api/kyc/health reports only configured/MISSING, .gitignore covers .env and kyc-store.json, and the GET session route deliberately projects five fields instead of proxying the decision document. And the colour system holds up — I computed muted-on-surface at ~7.4:1 dark and ~5.5:1 light, with both accent pairings above 5:1. The accessibility problems here are keyboard, naming and type size, not contrast.

Suggested order: the two server crashes and /api auth first (they are remote and cheap to fix), then the nav() fix which clears two blockers at once, then mobile navigation, then the doctype/lang/h1 head block, then escaping. I would treat the empty .env values as a decision point rather than a fix — nothing about the KYC path should be exposed until the auth and rate limiting land.

# App design ideas (next build)

### [L] Commitment Ledger — the whole investment flow as one append-down transcript
**Where:** New route #/app/invest/{offerId}, launched from the offer detail 'Invest from EUR 100' button (currently a dead toast at app-prototype-v1.html:893). Replaces any idea of a modal wizard.

One scrolling page, max-width 720px, that appends steps downward like a terminal transcript rather than swapping screens. Seven numbered blocks: 01 ELIGIBILITY · 02 AMOUNT · 03 THRESHOLD CHECK · 04 KIIS ACKNOWLEDGEMENT · 05 SIGNATURE (e-ID) · 06 PAYMENT INSTRUCTION · 07 REFLECTION OPEN.

States per block (three, one class each):
- DONE — collapses to a single 32px .reg-row: mono index '01', label, right-aligned mono record 'PASSED 2026-05-12 · VALID TO 2028-05-12', hairline border, surface bg, 1px accent left-border. Click expands read-only.
- ACTIVE — full card, --surface, 1px --hairline, 18px padding, mono header 'STEP 03 / 07' at 10.5px .08em --ink-muted.
- LOCKED — dashed 1px --hairline, opacity 1 (never faded — legal copy must stay full contrast), body replaced by mono 'LOCKED — COMPLETE STEP 02'. The API is the real gate (lisa 19 §8); the UI only mirrors it.

Banned by construction: no percentage progress bar (a filling funnel is an urgency mechanic), no 'you're almost there', no step timer. The index 'STEP 03 / 07' is the only progress signal. Primary verb is 'Record commitment', never 'Invest now'. Sticky bottom action bar on mobile (<780px) holding only the active step's single primary button, 56px tall, above the fixed risk line (add 74px bottom offset, already the body padding).

A returning investor whose assessments are valid sees steps 01/04 pre-collapsed and lands on 02 — the flow shortens honestly without hiding that the checks happened.

On exit-without-completing: no 'are you sure', no saved-cart nudge. State persists silently; a mono line on the dashboard reads 'UNFINISHED COMMITMENT — LAINE METRICS OÜ · STARTED 2026-08-06'. No email reminder about it, ever (that is solicitation).

*Why:* The prototype has an entire regulated flow behind a toast. A ledger shell is the one structure that makes art 21's mandated friction read as competence rather than obstruction: each barrier finishes as a dated, signed record the investor can see accumulating. It also matches the register-terminal thesis better than a modal wizard, and it gives every subsequent idea (2,3,4,5,6,7) a home with consistent state grammar.

*Risk:* None if the progress indicator stays an index and never a percentage. The one thing to police in review: nobody adds a 'complete your investment' re-engagement email or badge — that turns an abandoned flow into solicitation under art 27.

### [M] Ticket field — amount entry as an instrument, with a live threshold readout and no slider
**Where:** Step 02 of the Commitment Ledger.

Input: 34px ui-monospace, tabular-nums, slashed-zero, right-aligned, with a static non-editable 'EUR' glyph in --ink-muted to its left. Thin-space thousand grouping applied on input using the same single formatter as the landing page. Bounds: min EUR 100, max = remaining allocation; typing beyond max clamps with a mono note 'REMAINING ALLOCATION EUR 162 500' — a plain figure, never 'only X left'.

Quick chips: 100 / 250 / 500 / 1 000 only. Deliberately no chips above the art 21(7) threshold, and no 'most popular' / 'typical ticket' label — either would be an inducement.

No slider. A slider's whole affordance is 'drag right for more'; a numeric field is neutral.

Below the field, a 4-row .reg-row readout with dotted leaders, recomputing on every keystroke:
  OF YOUR DECLARED NET WORTH            2.4%
  ART 21(7) THRESHOLD                   EUR 1 000   (higher of EUR 1 000 / 5% = EUR 900)
  YOUR TOTAL ON OSALUS AFTER THIS       EUR 1 550
  10% GUIDANCE, ALL PLATFORMS           EUR 18 000

Crossing the threshold does not gate the field. The threshold row flips from --ink-muted to --risk-ink on --risk-bg and gains mono text 'ABOVE THRESHOLD — A WARNING AND A SEPARATE CONSENT STEP WILL FOLLOW'. Forward disclosure, no ambush. The below-threshold state must never be green or ticked — 'safe amount' framing is advice.

Cumulative-position rule from lisa 19 §5.2: the threshold test also fires when the running Osalus total crosses it, even if this ticket alone does not. Row 3 exists precisely so that firing is never a surprise.

If the loss simulation is stale (>14 months) the readout rows render as '—' with mono 'NET WORTH FIGURE EXPIRED — UPDATE THE SIMULATION' and step 02 cannot complete.

*Why:* This is where the platform either respects the investor or nudges them. Showing the threshold arithmetic before it bites converts a blocking wall into a stated rule, which is exactly the register-terminal voice — and it kills the two default fintech patterns (slider, 'most investors put in EUR 2 000') that would be art 27 findings.

*Risk:* The 10%-guidance row must be labelled 'all platforms' and 'guidance', per lisa 19 §4.3 — worded as a limit set by Osalus it becomes a false statement about the firm's own rules.

### [M] Art 21(7) consent gate — a consent record you visibly sign, with 'Reduce' first
**Where:** Step 03 of the Commitment Ledger, rendered as a full-viewport blocking layer.

Blocking per lisa 19 §5.3: no close X, no scrim click-through, no Esc, no scroll-past; `inert` on everything behind it; focus trapped; the fixed risk line stays visible beneath.

Header: mono 11px .08em 'RISK WARNING — REGULATION (EU) 2020/1503, ART 21(7)'.
Amount block: 30px mono 'EUR 2 500' + muted mono '= 5.4% OF YOUR DECLARED NET WORTH (EUR 46 000)'.
Six mandated statements as hairline-separated 15px rows in --ink (never --ink-muted, never below 14px): exceeds the level we must warn about; you may lose the entire amount; you cannot easily sell it; not covered by deposit guarantee or investor compensation; you should not invest more than 10% of your net worth in crowdfunding across all platforms; please reconsider the amount.

Comprehension (§5.4.2): three questions drawn from THIS offer's KIIS, each a radio triple, each footed by its source chip 'KIIS § B.3'. Wrong answers do not lock the investor out — they re-render with mono 'READ KIIS § B.3 AND ANSWER AGAIN' plus an inline OPEN link. No score shown.

Single dedicated checkbox concerning only this warning (§5.4.1) — it must not be bundled with terms, privacy, or anything else.

Confirmation phrase (§5.4.3): input requiring the exact string generated from the amount — `I ACCEPT THE RISK OF LOSING EUR 2 500`. Generated per-amount so it can never become muscle memory. Case-insensitive, whitespace-normalised, but the digits must match.

Actions, equal visual weight, reduce-first in DOM and reading order:
  [ Reduce the amount ]  (ghost, always enabled, returns to step 02 with the field focused)
  [ Confirm and continue ]  (solid, disabled until checkbox + 3 correct + exact phrase)

Footer stamp, mono 10px, using the signature-stamp object from 17-design-elements: 'THIS CONSENT IS RECORDED — CONTENT SHA-256 a3f9c1…e77b · 2026-08-06 11:42 UTC · YOUR THREE ANSWERS · KIIS v1.2 · DEVICE + IP'. Plus one line: 'A PREVIOUS CONSENT IS NEVER REUSED. THIS ONE APPLIES ONLY TO THIS INVESTMENT.' (§5.4 final clause.)

*Why:* Every retail platform builds this as a dismissible amber banner. Rendering it as a signed record — showing the investor the exact hash-and-timestamp evidence being written — turns the single most annoying compliance moment into the strongest trust moment on the platform, and it is the one screen a Finantsinspektsioon reviewer will look at hardest.

*Risk:* The failure mode is subtle: if 'Confirm' is solid-accent and 'Reduce' is a text link, the screen becomes a dark pattern that nudges past a statutory warning. Reduce must be a full ghost button, listed first. Second risk: the three questions must come from the offer's own KIIS, not a generic bank, or the 'evidence of understanding this investment' requirement is not met.

### [L] Knowledge test — an exam sheet, not a quiz game
**Where:** New route #/app/assessment/test. Entered from the first-run readiness ledger, from an offer's unmet-gate CTA, and on 2-year expiry.

Layout: ruled sheet, max-width 680px. One question per block: mono index 'Q07 / 18' 10.5px muted, question 15px --ink, options as full-width hairline radio rows, 44px min height, hover border-color→accent 150ms.

Hard rules from lisa 19 §3.3: correct answers are never shown before or during; no per-question right/wrong feedback; no score meter while answering; no timer; no streak, no confetti, no 'nice!'. Questions randomly drawn from a pool of ≥60. Learning materials are linked only from the result screen and the empty state — never inside the sheet (the regulation requires them presented separately).

Left rail (desktop ≥900px): an 18-cell sheet map, each cell a 20px hairline square that fills --surface-raised when answered and gains a 1px accent border when it is the current question. This is a state map, not a progress bar — no percentage, no 'x of y complete' encouragement. Below 900px it becomes a single horizontal scroll strip.

Critical questions (total loss, illiquidity, no guarantee scheme) carry NO visual mark during the test — marking them leaks the answer key's weight. They are named only on the result screen.

Result, two states:
- PASSED (≥70% and all critical correct): signature-stamp object, mono 'ASSESSMENT PASSED · 2026-08-06 · VALID TO 2028-08-06', then a kv of section-level results and the learning-material links.
- NOT PASSED: the art 21(4) warning as its own blocking panel, 15px --ink: 'Based on your answers, the crowdfunding services offered on this platform may not be appropriate for you.' Then the full risk statement, then a single dedicated acknowledgement checkbox, then 'Continue anyway' (ghost) and 'Retake the assessment' (solid — the encouraged path is the safe one). Acknowledgement writes content hash + timestamp. Beneath: mono dated fact 'RETAKE AVAILABLE AFTER 2026-08-07 11:42 UTC · 2 OF 3 ATTEMPTS REMAINING IN THIS 30-DAY WINDOW'.

Expiry banner on the dashboard at T-60 days: mono 'KNOWLEDGE ASSESSMENT EXPIRES 2026-09-30 — RETAKE TO KEEP INVESTING'. Static; no colour escalation as the date nears.

*Why:* The knowledge test is the first thing a new investor meets and it sets the platform's whole tone. Gamified quiz furniture (progress %, ticks, streaks) would train the exact click-through behaviour the regulation is trying to prevent, and would read as unserious to the tech audience. An exam sheet with a stamped result is the same design argument as the register extract on the landing page.

*Risk:* Two: (1) any inline learning content next to a question breaches §3.3's separation requirement; (2) 'Continue anyway' must not be visually subordinate to 'Retake' to the point of being hidden — the right to proceed after acknowledgement is statutory, so it stays a real, findable ghost button.

### [M] Loss simulation — the '10% line' figure block, declared in ranges only
**Where:** New route #/app/assessment/simulation. Re-prompted at 12 months, blocking at 14 (lisa 19 §4.4).

Three input groups — INCOME · ASSETS · LIABILITIES — each a row of hairline segmented buttons carrying EUR bands, never free-number fields (§4.2 requires ranges, data minimisation). Segments: 8-10 bands each, wrapping to a grid below 600px, 44px tall, selected state = 1px accent border + --surface-raised, mono label. A sub-question per group for the qualifying detail the regulation names (income permanent vs temporary; assets split incl. pension and property; liabilities incl. future commitments).

Live result block, using the landing page's subscription-figure typography:
  line 1: 'EUR 18 000' at 30px mono --ink, followed by ' = 10% OF NET WORTH EUR 180 000' at the same size in --ink-muted (the slash/equals construction reads as an instrument register)
  line 2, 15px --ink, verbatim per §4.3: 'This is the amount whose total loss you should be able to bear. It is guidance for your own consideration — not a limit set by Osalus. You should not invest more than 10% of your net worth in crowdfunding projects across all platforms.'

The single permitted graphic: one 20px hairline-bordered horizontal bar = net worth at full width, with the leftmost 10% filled --accent, a 1px --ink tick at the boundary and a mono flag '10% LINE'. It renders at final width at layout — no fill animation. An animated filling bar here would read as a gauge to top up.

*Risk:* One-way ratchet risk: never phrase the output as 'you can invest up to EUR 18 000'. It is a loss-bearing capacity, not an allowance — the difference is the whole legal point, and the copy above is written to be shipped verbatim.

### [M] Reflection window — a counter that only ever points at withdrawal
**Where:** Persistent account banner under the topbar on every investor view while any commitment is in reflection; plus a T+ rail on the commitment detail page; plus the portfolio row.

Lisa 19 §6.3 mandates a persistent banner with a counter, so this must count — the design job is making the counter point at a protective action, not a purchase. Every rule below follows from that.

Banner: full-width, --surface-raised, 1px --hairline top and bottom, NOT --risk-bg (amber is reserved for the risk warning; spending it here dilutes it and frames the investor's own protection as a hazard). 'REFLECTION PERIOD OPEN — LAINE METRICS OÜ · EUR 2 500 · ENDS 2026-08-10 09:12 UTC · 2 DAYS 14 HOURS REMAINING'
Resolution is minutes, never seconds. A ticking seconds field is trading-floor grammar and the one thing that would make this read as a countdown. The counter never changes colour, weight, or size as it shrinks. Banned strings anywhere near it: hurry, last chance, expires soon, act now, don't miss.
Right: [ Withdraw ] as a ghost button and 'View commitment' as a link. Withdraw is the only CTA the banner ever carries.

Withdraw behaviour (§6.4 — this is a code-review rule, not a preference): ONE click. No confirmation modal. No reason field. No alternative offer, no 'are you sure you want to give up this opportunity', no pause-instead option. The codebase must not contain a retention path on this route. Result is a neutral mono confirmation: 'COMMITMENT WITHDRAWN 2026-08-08 10:04 UTC · FUNDS RETURNED TO YOUR SOURCE ACCOUNT WITHIN 5 BUSINESS DAYS · NO FEE APPLIED' plus an immediate email.

The resolution is that the countdown runs toward the loss of a protection, and the only button attached to it is the protective one — which is a mechanic the regulator actively wants prominent. Getting this one right is what makes the 'no urgency' claim credible everywhere else.

*Risk:* Two named: (1) minute resolution and constant colour are load-bearing, not cosmetic — a seconds ticker or a reddening bar recreates exactly the urgency the system forbids; (2) the no-confirmation rule will look like a bug to a future engineer and get 'fixed'. The composer will not submit without one of the two. Answers are immutable; edits append 'EDITED 2026-08-05 — PREVIOUS VERSION RETAINED'. Unanswered questions stay visible as 'AWAITING ANSWER · ASKED 2026-08-04' so silence is on the record. Header microlabel: 'ANSWERS ARE RECORDS. THEY MAY NOT ADD INFORMATION THAT IS NOT IN THE KIIS.'

Right rail — the invest panel. Min EUR 100, then the investor's own four gates as a live checklist with dates (Terms ✓ 2026-08-06 · Identity ✓ · Knowledge assessment ✓ valid to 2028-05 · Loss simulation ✓ valid to 2027-08). If a gate is unmet the primary button is REPLACED by that gate's specific CTA ('Take the 10-minute assessment'), never a dead disabled button. Below the button, within 24px, the no-advice line at full contrast.

*Why:* The offer page is where the money decision happens and it is currently thinner than the landing page's illustrative card. Three things are missing that investors actually need — what document governs this, what do I end up owning, and who else asked what — and each of them is a compliance obligation that doubles as the most persuasive honest content available. The KIIS version history and the always-visible unanswered questions are the two details competitors will not copy.

*Risk:* The cap-table 'your 0.048%' line is the one to watch: it must stay pure arithmetic on the stated terms. The moment it acquires a EUR value it becomes a valuation of unlisted shares and an art 27 problem.

### [M] Community channel — pinned KIIS, real role ledger, founder pre-check composer, report-to-case
**Where:** #/app/messages community channels; #/company/community; and the flag path into admin.

Channel header, top to bottom: (1) the KIIS document object in one-line compact form, pinned above everything — filename + version + 'OPEN ↗'; (2) the existing --risk-bg pinned rule line, kept verbatim; (3) the feed.

Roles as chips, four only: FOUNDER (accent) · COMPANY TEAM · INVESTOR · OSALUS MODERATOR (accent, hairline-outlined). Member rail shows 'MEMBER SINCE 2026-08-04' per person, never their commitment amount or whether they invested at all — the channel must not become a leaderboard, and holdings are personal data.

Founder composer with a client-side pre-check strip: as they type, a mono advisory line below the box lights when it matches the forbidden-pattern lexicon — projection/return language (%, 'return', 'x', 'IRR', 'we expect', 'by 2028 we will'), urgency ('last chance', 'closing soon', 'only … left', 'hurry'), and comparative superlatives ('the best', 'the only'). It does NOT block (client-side matching cannot be authoritative and a false block is worse than a flag). It shows 'PRE-CHECK — 2 PHRASES FLAGGED · REVIEW BEFORE POSTING' with the phrases underlined 1px accent in a preview. Coaching copy, not scolding: "'we expect 3x by 2028' is a projection. There is no projection in the KIIS to cite — remove it, or cite the KIIS section the claim comes from." On post, any flags travel with the message into the admin art-27 queue.

Report affordance on every post (hover/focus, and always visible on touch): a 12px ghost 'Report' opening a compact form — reason chips (RETURN CLAIM · URGENCY · NOT IN THE KIIS · OFF TOPIC · ABUSE) plus an optional 200-char note. On submit the reporter sees a case number: 'REPORTED — CASE 2026-041. YOU WILL RECEIVE A DATED WRITTEN REPLY.' Extends the complaints-register motif into the social surface.

Moderation outcome must be visible, never silent: a removed post leaves a tombstone row in place — hairline dashed, mono, --ink-muted: 'POST REMOVED BY COMPLIANCE 2026-08-05 · REASON: RETURN CLAIM · CASE 2026-041'. Silent deletion breaks the append-only promise the whole product makes.

Empty channel state: 'No posts yet. Founders post updates here; investors ask questions. For anything about the offer's terms, the KIIS above is the governing document.'

*Why:* The community is the platform's largest art 27 exposure — it is the one surface where an unsupervised person makes claims about a live offer to retail investors. Building the pre-check into the composer moves enforcement from after-the-fact moderation to authoring time, which is both cheaper and the only version that scales, and the tombstone rule keeps moderation itself auditable.

*Risk:* Two: (1) the lexicon will produce false positives — it must advise, never block, or founders will route around the platform; (2) the tombstone must not quote the removed text, or removal fails to remove anything.

**Verdict:** The app prototype is a solid shell with a hole in the middle: the entire regulated investment flow — the thing the licence is actually for — is one toast at app-prototype-v1.html:893. Everything else (auth, terms gate, rail, community, admin tables) is scaffolding around an absent core. So the 11 moves are weighted hard toward the flow: ideas 1-7 build it end to end (ledger shell → amount → art 21(7) gate → knowledge test → loss simulation → segregated-account payment → reflection window), 8-9 rebuild the offer page and community into record-keeping surfaces, 10 makes four-eyes a visible object instead of a toast, 11 fixes first-run.

The single hardest design problem is the reflection period (idea 7): lisa 19 §6.3 mandates a persistent counter, the design system forbids countdowns. The resolution — the counter runs toward the loss of a right, and the only CTA attached to it is Withdraw — is what makes the whole no-urgency claim defensible, and it carries three rules that are load-bearing rather than stylistic: minute resolution not seconds, constant colour as it shrinks, and no confirmation modal on the withdraw path (§6.4 forbids retention flows outright — that one needs a code comment or a future engineer will "fix" it).

Two defects found in the current file that should be fixed regardless of which ideas get built:

1. The dashboard prints a portfolio value "EUR 248.00" (lines 387, 845) while the portfolio page states "No value estimates are shown: unlisted shares have no market price" (line 1020). The screens contradict each other, and the tile is a valuation of unlisted shares — an art 27 exposure. Replace with a share count and delete the `val` field from the holdings model.

2. Design-system drift: the prototype has quietly introduced `--ok: #4DB380` and `--warn: #D9A441` (line 8), neither of which exists in the locked token table in 15-master-design.md. The green is the bigger problem — green/red are the performance-signalling colours the system deliberately excludes, and `.tag.ok` is now used for APPROVED, PASSED, ACTIVE, SETTLED across every admin table. Amber is also reserved for the risk line, yet `.tag.warn` currently marks the investor's own reflection period (line 1018), framing a statutory protection as a hazard. Recommend collapsing both to hairline/accent/risk-token states before more surfaces inherit them.

Three specs are blocked on facts nobody has yet: the PSP name and IBAN (idea 6 — still an open blocker per 14-critique-next-actions.md §1; ship it with the build-time placeholder guard, never a mock IBAN), the real AureviaFund OÜ registry code, and the ET/EN official risk-warning strings. Each already has a documented fail-loud pattern in 17-design-elements — reuse it rather than inventing placeholder data that survives into a screenshot.
