# ECSPR register sweep — FI/ESMA, who is actually licensed

## Strategic insights

- THE ESTONIAN ECSPR EQUITY NICHE IS EMPTY, CONFIRMED FROM THE PRIMARY REGISTER: after Finantsinspektsioon revoked Crowdestate's licence at the company's own request on 13/04/2026, FI's active register shows exactly ONE Estonian-authorised CSP (Estateguru OÜ — real-estate LENDING). Zero Estonian equity CSPs exist; the client would be the first and only one.
- The gap extends across the whole target region: of the ~24 CSPs authorised in EE+LV+LT+FI (EE 1, LV 7, LT ~15, FI 1), only ONE does equity under ECSPR — CrowdedHero (LV), which is sub-scale and cannot replicate the Estonian OÜ notarisation-waiver/direct-register-title mechanic because it operates under Latvian company law. Lithuania's 15 CSPs are effectively 100% loan-based; Finland's only CSP (Vauraus) is loan-based, while Finnish equity crowdfunding (Springvest, 10k+ retail investors) runs under a costly MiFID wrapper at EUR 2-6M ticket sizes — leaving the EUR 150-800k SME equity segment unserved in all four launch markets.
- The emptiness is partly a demand warning, not just an opportunity: Crowdestate exited citing five years of adverse conditions (COVID, inflation, rate spikes, war, recession) collapsing retail appetite, and Estateguru is dragging ~EUR 96M of defaulted loans through recovery. EU-wide (ESMA, 2024 data published Jan 2026), equity is the SMALLEST funding type by invested amount and investor count — but average equity project size (EUR 640k) sits exactly in the client's EUR 150-800k target band, and retail average tickets (~EUR 660) validate the EUR-0-investor-fee, volume-based model.
- Finantsinspektsioon process (verified live from fi.ee): application in ESTONIAN via the taotlus.fi.ee portal (MANDATORY since 18/03/2026), EUR 1,000 menetlustasu, 18 documentation categories per Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/2112 (business plan, governance, ICT/cybersecurity, business continuity, management fit-and-proper, conflict-of-interest/related-party exclusion, complaint handling, payment arrangements, KIIS verification procedures, non-sophisticated-investor safeguards), completeness check within 25 WORKING days, decision within 3 months of a COMPLETE application (realistic elapsed time 6-9 months), plus national overlays: Reklaamiseadus (advertising), Tarbijakaitseseadus, Keeleseadus. Art 18 passporting to FI/LV/LT is a free notification with a 15-calendar-day clock — the cheapest pan-Baltic licence in EU finance.
- FI is experienced but under-employed in this vertical: it has authorised two CSPs (Crowdestate 2023, Estateguru 2023) and processed one voluntary revocation (2026), and now supervises a single CSP. A well-prepared equity application will get senior attention and there is institutional interest in the domestic market not dying — but expect heavy scrutiny precisely on the novel elements: the platform-run cap-table layer (frame it strictly as evidentiary, legal title in the Business Register), the art 25 bulletin board (no matching engine, non-binding reference price), and the OÜ-share 'admitted instruments' classification. ESMA's recent clarification on nominee structures in equity crowdfunding is favourable context: the client's DIRECT-title model is the conservative end of the spectrum.
- EU-wide market context for the application's market-analysis section: 181 authorised CSPs across 21 member states (ESMA market report, 2024 data, published 14/01/2026; industry trackers count ~188 active mid-2026). France (~50), Italy, Spain dominate; providers in the top 5 countries take 80%+ of volume. Over half of EU volume is loan-based. ESMA's own data shows Estonia has one of the HIGHEST cross-border investment shares in the EU (~78% of amounts invested via Estonian providers come from abroad) — the single best statistic supporting an Estonia-seat + passport-everywhere distribution strategy.
- Foreign equity giants are already passported INTO Estonia and it doesn't matter: Crowdcube Europe (CNMV) and Seedrs/Republic Europe (Central Bank of Ireland) have been on FI's cross-border list since 2023 with ~zero Estonian SME originations — because their nominee-based, venture-startup model doesn't serve profitable EUR 0.5-3M-revenue companies selling 5-15%. The FI cross-border list (~36 entities) is noise: passport notifications are free, local operations are not.
- Timing: regional NCAs are issuing at an accelerating clip in LENDING (Latvijas Banka: 4 new CSPs Jul 2025-Mar 2026), so regulator bandwidth exists and the equity slot is unlikely to stay open indefinitely — the most plausible fast-follower threats are CrowdedHero deepening into Estonia, Invesdor re-entering the Nordics/Baltics with equity via passport, or a Lithuanian lender pivoting. Filing a complete application in H2 2026 targets authorisation around mid-2027 with no visible competing Estonian equity application in the pipeline.
- Positioning writes itself against every incumbent: vs Estateguru/Profitus (lending, default-scarred) — a different asset class with direct ownership; vs Funderbeam/Springvest (MiFID) — 10x lighter cost structure and direct Business Register title instead of nominee/SPV layers; vs Crowdcube/Seedrs (passported equity) — local law engineering (>=EUR 10k paid-in capital + articles waiving notarised share-transfer form) that makes fully digital OÜ share subscription AND insolvency-proof direct holdings possible, which none of them can copy without becoming an Estonian-law platform.

## Platforms

### Estateguru OÜ (Estonia)
- **Country:** Estonia
- **Licence:** ECSPR authorised by Finantsinspektsioon 08/05/2023. As of Feb 2026 it is the ONLY Estonian-authorised CSP on FI's active register.
- **Model:** Lending-based crowdfunding — property-backed (mortgage) business loans. NOT equity.
- **Instruments:** Secured loans (real estate collateral); no shares, no admitted instruments
- **Fees:** Investor side historically free for investing (secondary market/withdrawal fees added later); borrower pays origination ~2.5-4% (not re-verified this pass)
- **Volumes:** EUR 952M lifetime lending by end-June 2026, 78% (EUR 744M) repaid; ~EUR 95.7M stuck in recovery in exited markets (Germany, Finland); EUR 70M recovered from defaults over platform history (EUR 7.5M in 2025, EUR 6.3M in H1 2026)
- **Strengths:** Largest Baltic crowdfunding brand, pan-Baltic + passported EU-wide; Survived the 2023-2025 default crisis and kept its licence; Very high cross-border investor share (ESMA data shows ~78% of Estonian-provider investment volume is cross-border — proof EU-wide retail distribution from Estonia works)
- **Weaknesses:** Severe reputational damage from German/Finnish loan defaults (reviews titled 'Disaster'); Pure lending — does not touch the equity niche at all; Spent EUR 1.7M of own funds on recoveries in 2025 — drag on P&L
- **Notes:** Not a direct competitor to an equity platform, but defines Estonian retail investors' (partly negative) mental model of 'ühisrahastus'. Its cross-border stats are the best evidence for the client's passporting thesis.

### Crowdestate AS (Estonia) — DEFUNCT as CSP
- **Country:** Estonia
- **Licence:** ECSPR licence granted 06/03/2023 by Finantsinspektsioon; WITHDRAWN at the company's own request 13/04/2026 (application filed 10/04/2026 per FI notice). Retains a payment institution licence and wants to continue as a payment institution.
- **Model:** Was real-estate lending/mezzanine crowdfunding (occasional equity-like deals)
- **Instruments:** Real estate loans
- **Fees:** n/a (winding down crowdfunding; platform stays accessible to existing investors during run-off)
- **Volumes:** ~EUR 135M+ cumulative historic (pre-decline); volumes collapsed — management said continuation was no longer reasonable
- **Strengths:** Was Estonia's oldest real-estate crowdfunding platform (2014)
- **Weaknesses:** Exited citing 5 years of adverse conditions: COVID, inflation, rate spikes, Ukraine war, prolonged recession killing retail appetite for real-estate crowdfunding
- **Notes:** STRATEGIC SIGNAL, two-sided: (1) its exit means Estonia now has exactly ONE licensed CSP and ZERO equity CSPs — the niche is empty; (2) its stated exit reasons are a demand-side warning for any Estonian retail-investment platform. Its 'CSP + payment institution' combo is also a data point on Estonian PSP structuring.

### Funderbeam (Funderbeam Markets AS, Estonia) — NOT ECSPR
- **Country:** Estonia
- **Licence:** NOT on the ECSPR register. Holds an Estonian investment firm (MiFID) licence from Finantsinspektsioon (Funderbeam Markets AS); group also has UK/other entities. Active as of mid-2026.
- **Model:** Equity fundraising + secondary trading marketplace for private companies — functionally the closest Estonian equity competitor, but regulated as a MiFID investment firm, not a CSP
- **Instruments:** Syndicated/nominee structures with tradable 'tokens'/notes representing equity exposure; own secondary marketplace with live matching
- **Fees:** Fundraiser success fee + investor trading fees (schedule varies; not re-verified)
- **Volumes:** Not disclosed recently; historically tens of EUR millions across 100+ raises; pivoted increasingly toward secondary liquidity and B2B services
- **Strengths:** Real secondary market with matching engine (something an ECSPR art 25 bulletin board legally cannot be); Estonian brand recognition among startup investors
- **Weaknesses:** Nominee/SPV layer — investors do not hold direct title in the business register (exact opposite of the client's direct-registry-title differentiator); MiFID cost base; drifted away from small-ticket local SME equity
- **Notes:** Differentiation line for the application/marketing: Funderbeam = indirect holdings + real exchange; client = direct Business Register title, admitted instruments (OÜ shares), evidentiary cap-table only, art 25 bulletin board. No overlap in regulatory regime.

### Fundwise (Estonia) — dormant
- **Country:** Estonia
- **Licence:** Never obtained ECSPR authorisation; not on any register; effectively inactive (site still up, no meaningful recent activity found)
- **Model:** Was Estonia's first equity crowdfunding platform (founded 2015, pre-ECSPR national regime)
- **Instruments:** OÜ/AS shares via subscription (pre-ECSPR era)
- **Fees:** n/a
- **Volumes:** ~EUR 10M-range cumulative across ~60 raises historically (stale data)

- **Weaknesses:** Failed to make the ECSPR jump — the compliance lift killed the pre-regulation local players
- **Notes:** The graveyard entry: Estonia's national-regime equity platforms (Fundwise) died at the ECSPR transition rather than converting. The licence IS the moat.

### SIA CrowdedHero Latvia

- **Instruments:** Equity in SIA/SA companies via subscription; fixed-income notes

- **Volumes:** Small: single-digit EUR millions cumulative across a modest number of campaigns (no 2025 total published; niche scale)

- **Weaknesses:** Low volumes and low brand awareness in Estonia; Latvian company-law base — cannot offer the Estonian OÜ digital share-transfer-waiver mechanic; Deal flow thin
- **Notes:** The nearest true competitor by regime+model, yet structurally unable to replicate the client's core differentiator (Estonian ÄS §149 notarisation-waiver + Business Register direct title). Watch it; don't fear it.
