# Western-European equity majors — benchmarks

## Strategic insights

- The 2025-26 shakeout is real and recent: WiSEED (ECSPR-licensed, EUR 500m+ track record) went through judicial restructuring in Oct-Dec 2025 and was absorbed by a real-estate group, and OneCrowd/Seedmatch filed for insolvency on 27 July 2026. Both were primary-issuance-only models. Meanwhile Crowdcube posted its first-ever full-year profit (FY2025) explicitly because secondaries grew to ~50% of its business. Lesson: plan the art 25 bulletin board as a core revenue and retention product from day one, not a compliance afterthought.
- Nominee custody is the single mechanic that powered Crowdcube's and Seedrs/Republic's growth (450k+ shareholders under one nominee; issuer-free secondary settlement; GBP 2k/yr recurring nominee fees) — and it is structurally unavailable to the client: holding client securities requires custody authorisation outside the plain ECSPR licence and contradicts the no-custody design. The client's substitute stack (>=EUR 10k paid-in capital + articles waiving notarised form + digitally signed transfers + Business Register title + evidentiary cap-table layer) is the only credible direct-holding equivalent in the EU, and no benchmark platform has it. Market it as such: 'your shares survive the platform' is precisely what OneCrowd's stranded investors lack.
- Because settlement without a nominee cannot be T+0 book-entry, design the secondary bulletin board around asynchronous completion: match on the board (non-binding reference price, art 25-compliant), then auto-generate the digitally signed transfer instrument and register filing, targeting settlement in days. Republic Europe's 2025 shift from monthly week-long trading windows to a 24/7/365 bulletin board shows continuous boards are now the UX baseline — skip the trading-cycle era entirely.
- Investor-side fees are the incumbents' weak flank and the client's cleanest differentiator: Crowdcube charges 2.49% entry + 5% of profit at exit; Republic Europe 2.5% entry + 5% profit fee + 2% each side on secondaries; Startupxplore up to 6% entry + 20% carry (and its EUR 23m in 12 years shows investor-pays kills volume); WiSEED layered entry, management and recovery fees onto a defaulting book. Only Companisto and Sego Venture are free at entry, and Companisto still takes 15% carry. 'EUR 0 investor fees, ever, including exit' beats every benchmark — put it on the homepage.
- But the issuer-pays-only comparators show the revenue risk: Sego Venture (EUR 1.5k + 6%, investors free) produced only ~EUR 40m over 15 years and survives on adjacent verticals; Seedmatch (5-10% issuer-only) went insolvent. Crowdcube needed 13 loss-making years and a secondary-fee engine to break even, and Republic's GBP 2k annual nominee fee is the only recurring issuer revenue in the set. Recommendation: keep EUR 0 investor fees but build recurring issuer-side revenue (annual listed-company/cap-table administration fee, secondary-transfer processing fee charged to the issuer or seller-side project owner, paid promotion/investor-update tooling).
- Auto-invest does not exist on any of the six equity benchmarks — for securities it would constitute individual portfolio management outside the ECSPR investment-crowdfunding perimeter (it exists only on lending platforms). Its absence is not a competitive gap; do not build it. Likewise Republic's 2026 'Mirror Notes' (rSPAX SpaceX-exposure notes) and Crowdcube's PISCES access are MiFID/UK-venue plays outside an ECSPR licence — explicitly out of scope, and useful to cite to Finantsinspektsioon as things the client will NOT do.
- VC/angel co-investment is the highest-ROI mechanic that IS fully compatible with direct shareholding: Startupxplore's 'invest on the same terms as the lead' and Companisto's Angel Club (EUR 10k+ direct GmbH shares, 'a central contact for follow-on rounds') are the two strongest curation signals in the set. Copy both: display named lead/anchor investors and their committed amounts on each offer page and in the KIIS, and offer a higher-minimum tier. Companisto's Angel Club also proves demand for register-entry direct ownership at EUR 10k+ tickets — the client extends that to all ticket sizes.
- Germany is strategically open: Companisto deliberately sits outside ECSPR (BaFin national regime, no passport) and OneCrowd is insolvent, so the EU's largest economy has no passportable, profitable equity platform — while Crowdcube (CNMV, 2022) and Seedrs Europe (Central Bank of Ireland, Oct 2023) prove the single-licence pan-EU playbook the client is following via Finantsinspektsioon. An Estonian ECSPR licence with art 18 passporting into FI/LV/LT faces no entrenched licensed local incumbent in any of those markets.
- WiSEED's collapse specifically vindicates two client exclusions: real-estate/passive asset vehicles (the asset class whose 0.88%->28.22% delay explosion sank it) and interposed SPV/holding layers (the structure that let it charge investor-side management fees and made investor positions dependent on the platform's solvency). Keep both exclusions verbatim in the licence application's programme of operations — they are answerable, evidence-backed risk-mitigation choices.
- Fee benchmark for the application and pitch: the client's ~EUR 1,000 listing + ~6% success fee sits at or below every survivor (Crowdcube 7% + 0.75-1.5% completion; Republic 6% + ~GBP 12k fixed + GBP 2k/yr; Companisto 15% + 0.65% p.a.; Sego 6% + EUR 1.5k) — defensible as market-standard, with headroom to add a completion or annual admin fee later without becoming an outlier. The target-issuer profile (3+ years, EUR 0.5-3m revenue, EUR 150-800k raises) also sits in a gap: UK platforms chase later-stage brands and secondaries, German platforms chase startups with loans/carry — no benchmark focuses on digital-native equity for established SMEs.

## Platforms

### Crowdcube (UK + Crowdcube Europe S.L.)
- **Country:** UK; EU entity in Spain
- **Licence:** UK: FCA. EU: Crowdcube Europe S.L. — first platform authorised under ECSPR, by Spain's CNMV, April 2022. 2026: partnership with LSEG as retail gateway to PISCES (UK-regulated intermittent private-share venue — UK only, not an ECSPR structure).
- **Model:** Equity crowdfunding, pivoting hard into secondary liquidity / 'private markets infrastructure'. Acquired secondary-deal startup Semper (2023); Cubex company-led secondary events. Co-CEO (Apr 2026): secondaries could be up to 50% of entire business this year. Verticals: consumer brands, fintech (Monzo, Chip, Atom Bank), plus brokered blocks in Mistral, Perplexity, Bolt.
- **Instruments:** Ordinary/B shares held via Crowdcube Nominees Ltd (single nominee on cap table); direct shareholding historically only for large tickets. EU offers via ECSPR admitted instruments.
- **Fees:** RAISERS: no listing fee; 7% success fee (excl VAT) + completion fee ~0.75–1.5% of funds raised. INVESTORS: 2.49% investment fee (min £2.49, cap £250) + 5% success fee on investor profit at exit — i.e. investors DO pay, on both entry and exit.
- **Volumes:** £1.5bn+ cumulative invested; 450,000+ individual shareholders across 1,000+ companies; nominee holds £1bn+/$1.5bn securities. FY2024: revenue £9.8m, loss £6.2m; FY2025: first full-year net profit, driven by ~$100m of secondaries processed in 18 months (tech.eu, Apr 2026). Largest UK/EU secondary by seller count: Moneybox event, 24,000 shareholders.
- **Strengths:** First ECSPR licence in the EU (CNMV) — proven passporting playbook; Nominee enables issuer-free secondary settlement at scale (Cubex, PISCES); Consumer-brand raises (Monzo, Chip) as customer-acquisition engine; Reached profitability 2025 — proof the model works only with a liquidity leg; Capped, transparent investor fee (£250 max)
- **Weaknesses:** Investor fees on entry AND exit (5% of profit) — persistent community backlash; Loss-making for 13+ years until secondaries scaled; Nominee = platform-dependent custody; UK PISCES route not replicable under ECSPR
- **Notes:** Key benchmark lesson: primary issuance alone never made money; the 2024→2025 swing to profit came from secondary liquidity. Its secondary machine depends entirely on the nominee holding legal title — forbidden under client's no-custody model; client's substitute is the notarisation-waiver articles + digitally signed transfers + art 25 bulletin board.

### Republic Europe (ex-Seedrs)
- **Country:** UK; EU entity in Ireland
- **Licence:** UK: Seedrs Limited, FCA FRN 550317. EU: Seedrs Europe Limited, ECSPR authorisation from Central Bank of Ireland (from October 2023; AMF white-list confirms passporting). Owned by US Republic since 2022; rebranded Republic Europe July 2024.
- **Model:** Equity crowdfunding with universal nominee + the most developed EU/UK secondary market. 2025: secondary market moved from a one-week-per-month trading cycle to a 24/7/365 bulletin board. 2026: distributing 'Mirror Notes' (e.g. rSPAX SpaceX-exposure notes ahead of SpaceX's June 2026 Nasdaq debut) — derivative-style products outside classic crowdfunding.
- **Instruments:** Shares held by Seedrs Nominee on behalf of investors in virtually all deals — company sees one shareholder; nominee handles votes, pre-emption, exits, and settles all secondary trades without issuer involvement.
- **Fees:** RAISERS: ~£5k pre-registration fee + £5k launch fee + 6% of funds raised + 0.5% payment processing + £2k ANNUAL nominee fee (recurring revenue). INVESTORS: 2.5% investment fee (min £5, cap £250); 5% profit fee on gains (exit or secondary); secondary market 2% transaction fee on BOTH buyer and seller (min £0.50).
- **Volumes:** Approaching £3bn raised cumulatively over ~2,400 rounds by 2025 — largest in Europe by cumulative volume.
- **Strengths:** Only true recurring-liquidity venue in EU crowdfunding (weekly→24/7 bulletin board); Nominee removes all issuer admin — key raiser-side selling point; Recurring issuer revenue (£2k/yr nominee fee) smooths the primary-issuance cycle; First-mover ECSPR licence in Ireland; genuine pan-EU deal flow
- **Weaknesses:** Heavy fixed fees for raisers (~£12k before success fee); Investor fee stack (entry + profit + secondary both sides) erodes retail trust; Secondary trading restricted to nominee-held shares only — direct holders excluded; US parent's strategy drift (Mirror Notes, tokenisation) blurs regulatory perimeter
- **Notes:** Secondary market is legally a bulletin board with platform-set reference valuations, not an MTF — the closest live template for the client's art 25 board. But its settlement (instant book-entry inside the nominee) is impossible without custody; client's version must settle via digitally signed share-transfer instruments into the Business Register, so design for T+days not T+0.

### Companisto
- **Country:** Germany
- **Licence:** Deliberately OUTSIDE ECSPR — operates under German national regime: Companisto GmbH (§34f GewO investment intermediary since 2012) + Companisto Wertpapier GmbH as BaFin tied agent for securities. Consequence: no EU passport. (ECCL 2025 study: Germany is the only major EU market still largely outside ECSPR.)
- **Model:** Pure equity since ~2020 (dropped subordinated loans). Two-tier: retail from €250 via pooling SPV; 'Angel Club' from €10,000 invests DIRECTLY in GmbH shares/stock, with access to Germany's INVEST/BAFA grant (state rebate for angels). Positions itself as startup-financing network, not mass crowdfunding.
- **Instruments:** GmbH shares and AS-equivalent stock. Retail pooled in SPV (one line on cap table, single contact for follow-on rounds); Angel Club members hold direct title. Voting bundled via pooling agreements.
- **Fees:** RAISERS: 15% success commission on funds raised + 0.65% p.a. administration fee on the funding sum. INVESTORS: zero upfront; Companisto takes 15% carry on distributed profits.
- **Volumes:** €376.7m invested across 481+ startups; 191,241 registered investors; €49m raised in 2025 (claims highest-volume equity crowdfunding platform in the EU region for the year).
- **Strengths:** Two-tier retail/angel structure — Angel Club (€10k min, direct shares) drives most volume; State-subsidy integration (INVEST grant) as an acquisition lever; Zero investor entry fees; aligned 15% carry; Informal internal marketplace gives some liquidity without a regulated venue
- **Weaknesses:** 15% raiser commission is the most expensive in Europe; No ECSPR licence → cannot passport; locked into DACH; Pooling SPV keeps investors off the register — title depends on platform-group entities; 'Secondary marketplace' is unregulated private matching, no reference pricing
- **Notes:** Closest fee philosophy to client on the investor side (free entry) but monetises via carry, which requires the pooling layer to collect — hard to replicate with direct shareholding and contractually zero investor fees. Its Angel Club direct-shareholding tier proves demand for register-entry ownership at €10k+ tickets, exactly the client's wedge.

### Seedmatch (OneCrowd Group, incl. Econeers, Mezzany)
- **Country:** Germany
- **Licence:** DEFUNCT/INSOLVENT: OneCrowd GmbH + OneCrowd Loans GmbH + OneCrowd Securities GmbH filed for insolvency at Dresden Local Court on 27 July 2026 (days before this research); provisional administrators (BBL Brockdorff) appointed; operations continue provisionally; payments on its own 2022 corporate bond suspended; 'structured investor process' seeking a buyer. Historically operated under German national regime (VermAnlG subordinated loans via OneCrowd Loans; securities via OneCrowd Securities), not ECSPR.
- **Model:** Germany's crowdinvesting pioneer (2011). Profit-participating subordinated loans (Nachrangdarlehen) and venture debt from €250, later digital securities/bonds — never genuine equity with title.
- **Instruments:** Subordinated profit-participating loans; digital bonds/securities. No shares, no register entry — purely contractual claims against issuers.
- **Fees:** RAISERS: 5–10% of amount raised, success-only. INVESTORS: free.
- **Volumes:** €70m+ from ~78,000 users across 179 rounds (Seedmatch brand, pre-consolidation; figures stale — platform consolidated into OneCrowd brand by 2025).
- **Strengths:** First-mover brand in German crowdinvesting; Issuer-pays-only fee model (like client's); Low €250 minimum
- **Weaknesses:** Subordinated-loan instrument gave investors worst-of-both-worlds risk (equity risk, debt upside); No liquidity mechanism ever built; Parent insolvency now clouds servicing of all outstanding investor contracts; Never obtained ECSPR licence — no passport, no exit path
- **Notes:** Cautionary benchmark: 15 years of operation, then insolvency (startbase.com/news/crowdinvesting-plattform-onecrowd-stellt-insolvenzantraege-fuer-drei-gesellschaften). Its collapse strands investors whose claims are administered by the platform — the strongest possible argument for the client's design where legal title sits in the Estonian Business Register and survives platform failure.

### WiSEED
- **Country:** France
- **Licence:** PSFP/ECSPR agrément from AMF, no. FP-2023-33 (Nov 2023) + PSI investment-firm status (ACPR). STATUS: placed in accelerated redressement judiciaire (court-supervised restructuring) by Toulouse commercial court 21 Oct 2025; takeover by Advenis (Paris real-estate group) approved 11 Dec 2025; agréments maintained for service continuity under new owner.
- **Model:** Founded 2008 as Europe's equity-crowdfunding pioneer; drifted into majority real-estate crowdfunding + renewables, equity/startups a minority line. Min ticket ~€100.
- **Instruments:** Equity held via a per-project holding SPV ('holding d'investissement') interposed between investors and the company; bonds for real estate. Investors hold SPV units, not company shares.
- **Fees:** INVESTORS: 0.9% entry fee on subscriptions under €1,000 (added on top), plus ~5% management/structuring fee around the holding SPV (deducted from investment) on equity deals; recovery/collection costs on defaulted deals passed to investors. RAISERS: success fee typically 5–10%.
- **Volumes:** €500m+ invested cumulatively since 2008. Portfolio quality collapsed: project delays rose from 0.88% to 28.22% across 2024–25; reviews report 50–80% of projects late/in insolvency; Trustpilot 2.3/5.
- **Strengths:** Early ECSPR/PSFP + PSI dual licence; Very low minimum ticket (€100); Longest track record in continental Europe
- **Weaknesses:** Real-estate concentration destroyed it when the French property market turned; SPV holding layer added fees, opacity, and platform dependency; Charging investors entry + management + recovery fees while defaults spiked destroyed trust; Ended in judicial restructuring; absorbed by a real-estate group
- **Notes:** Second cautionary tale of 2025–26 alongside OneCrowd. Validates two client choices: (1) excluding passive real-estate/asset vehicles, (2) no interposed SPV — WiSEED's holding structure is what allowed it to charge investor-side management fees, and also what made investor positions hostage to the platform's fate.

### Startupxplore
- **Country:** Spain
- **Licence:** CNMV-authorised PFP no. 18 (April 2017, under Spain's Law 5/2015); migrated to ECSPR — CNMV registry updated 15 Dec 2023. Still active in 2026 but small (recent deals: Inrobics, Avant Devices).
- **Model:** Curated syndicate co-investment: retail invests on the SAME terms as a professional lead investor who sets the deal and does due diligence. Min ticket €1,000. No secondary market, no auto-invest.
- **Instruments:** Direct participation alongside lead investor (SL shares), with platform-provided custody/administration service covered by the investor subscription fee.
- **Fees:** INVERTED MODEL — INVESTORS pay: subscription fee up to 6% of amount invested (charged only if campaign reaches ≥90% of target; covers DD, custody, legal docs) + success commission up to 20% (+VAT) of investor capital gains. RAISERS: flat fee only, up to €4,500 per successful campaign.
- **Volumes:** €23m total across 88 projects; 45,498 registered investors — an order of magnitude smaller than DE/FR/UK peers after 12 years.
- **Strengths:** Lead-investor co-investment as a quality signal — strongest curation mechanic in the set; Founder-friendly pricing (near-free raises) attracts deal flow; Early ECSPR migration
- **Weaknesses:** Investor-pays model (6% + 20% carry) suppressed retail demand — volumes prove it; €1,000 minimum excludes small retail; No liquidity, no engagement features; stagnant
- **Notes:** Natural benchmark for the client's later Spain entry. Its lead-investor mechanic is cheap to copy and fully compatible with direct shareholding (publish lead's committed amount and identity on each offer/KIIS page); its fee structure is the exact inverse of the client's and demonstrably the losing configuration.

### SegoFinance / Sego Venture (ex-SociosInversores)
- **Country:** Spain
- **Licence:** SociosInversores 2010 SL — CNMV-supervised PFP, ECSPR-licensed (per Crowdinform); part of SegoFinance group.
- **Model:** Multi-vertical retail investment group: Sego Venture (equity crowdfunding, ex-SociosInversores, running since 2010), Sego Factoring (invoice finance), Sego Real Estate, Sego Funds — cross-sells one investor base across products. Sego Venture also makes direct investments in listed startups.
- **Instruments:** Equity participations in Spanish SLs; success-only economics.
- **Fees:** INVESTORS: no direct fees ('implicit' in project pricing). RAISERS: €1,500 fixed pre-campaign + 6% success fee on funds raised, charged only on successful close.
- **Volumes:** Sego Venture: 160+ projects financed, €40m+ invested since inception.
- **Strengths:** Fee model closest to the client's (issuer pays €1.5k + 6%; investors free); Multi-vertical cross-sell keeps investors active between equity deals; Success-only pricing attracts founders
- **Weaknesses:** Modest equity volumes (~€40m over 15 years) — issuer-only 6% fee needs high deal throughput to sustain a platform; Equity is subsidised by the factoring/real-estate verticals; Limited transparency; no secondary market
- **Notes:** Proof-of-concept and warning for the client's monetisation: EUR 0 investor fees + ~6% issuer success fee is viable but generates thin, cyclical revenue; Sego survives on adjacent verticals the client has excluded. Argues for the client's EUR 1,000 listing fee plus potential recurring issuer-side services (annual cap-table/bulletin admin fee, cf. Republic's £2k/yr nominee fee) rather than ever touching investor pricing.
