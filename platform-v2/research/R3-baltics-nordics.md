# Equity platforms in FI/LV/LT (passporting targets)

## Strategic insights

- The passporting targets are close to EMPTY of equity-CSP competition. Finland's only domestic ECSP (Vauraus) is a lender; equity is served by one foreign-licensed passport (Invesdor/FMA Austria) and one non-ECSPR MiFID firm (Springvest). Latvia's only equity CSP (CrowdedHero) has raised ~EUR 600k in three years. Lithuania's 13 ECSPR platforms are 100% lending/real-estate — no domestic equity CSP exists at all. A properly-executed Estonian equity ECSP would enter all three markets against, at most, one weak direct competitor.
- Investor-fee structure is the sharpest attack vector in every market. Springvest charges investors 3-4% subscription fees; Invesdor 1.5% on equity; CrowdedHero stacks 1.25% admin + 5.5% dividend fee + 9% exit carry. The client's EUR-0-investor-fee model (all economics from the project owner: ~EUR 1,000 listing + ~6% success) undercuts every incumbent and should headline all passporting marketing.
- The client's target deal segment is structurally unserved. Springvest only runs 8-10 rounds/year at EUR 5-50M for deep-tech; Invesdor's Finnish equity run-rate is modest and DACH-focused; Baltic lenders serve established SMEs with debt only. Operating companies with EUR 0.5-3M revenue raising EUR 150-800k — the client's exact niche — currently have no equity venue in Finland, Latvia or Lithuania.
- Enter Lithuania first among the Baltics; treat Finland as the volume prize but a harder second. Lithuania combines the largest retail crowd-investing pool in the Baltics (EUR 278.4M across 500k+ transactions in 2024; Profitus alone: 10k+ active investors, EUR 98M in 12 months), the hottest startup/SME scene (record EUR 238M startup funding in 2025), a total absence of domestic equity CSPs, and an efficient regulator (EUR 710 fee, 3-month reviews). Latvia is even emptier but its investor pool and SME base are smaller. Finland has the deepest retail equity culture (17k active Springvest investors alone) but two entrenched brands.
- Speed and UX expectations are set by Baltic lending platforms, not by equity peers. Lithuanian investors fund loans in hours from EUR 10-100 minimums on mobile-first platforms (Profitus, FinBee). An equity entrant must match that onboarding smoothness — the client's fully digital OÜ share subscription (no notary) is the only equity mechanic in the region that can approach lending-platform UX, and this should be benchmarked against Profitus, not against CrowdedHero.
- The digital-transfer differentiator is strongest as an ESTONIAN-DEAL EXPORT story. The waived-notarisation OÜ mechanism only applies to Estonian companies; passporting therefore means selling Estonian SME deals to Finnish/Baltic investors first. A second phase can onboard Latvian/Lithuanian project owners via Estonian OÜ top-cos (e-Residency makes this routine) to import the same digital rails — but structure them as genuine operating groups to stay clear of the client's own passive-holdco/AIFMD exclusion.
- Direct register-of-record ownership beats every competing custody model in the region and survives platform failure. Republic Europe uses nominee custody; CrowdedHero mixes SPVs; Funderbeam uses marketplace structures; FundedByMe/Pepins' 2025 shutdown made platform-insolvency risk concrete for Nordic investors. 'Your shares live in the Estonian Business Register, not on our platform' is a differentiator no incumbent can copy without rebuilding their model.
- Secondary liquidity is becoming table stakes — but the bulletin board is enough for now. Republic Europe moved to a 24/7 secondary market (GBP 1M traded in May 2025); CrowdedHero has a secondary module; Funderbeam trades T+10min. No one offers liquidity in Baltic SME equity. The client's art 25 bulletin board plus notarisation-free written transfers is a genuinely tradable-in-practice answer that requires no MTF licence — market it as 'digitally transferable shares', not as an exchange.
- The graveyard defines the playbook's guardrails: FundedByMe/Pepins (closed Feb 2025) died of generalist low-curation listings and sub-scale cross-border volumes; CrowdedHero is stalling on scattered multi-country deal-picking without a home-market flywheel. The winning sequence is the Springvest lesson (curation discipline, 100% round completion, visible success-rate metric) applied to the smaller SME segment, with Estonia's home flywheel proven before switching on art 18 passports.
- Watch three specific moves: (1) Letsinvest (LT) had its licence expanded in April 2025 to securities placement + SPV operations — the first Lithuanian CSP drifting toward equity; (2) Capitalia (LV/pan-Baltic, ECSP since Nov 2023) already originates the client's exact target SMEs across all three Baltics and could add equity — consider partnering for deal flow before it becomes a competitor; (3) Invesdor appointed a Nordics head in Dec 2025 — a renewed Finnish push could close the window in the client's richest target market.

## Platforms

### Invesdor (Invesdor Group / Invesdor Oy origin, now Helsinki-Vienna-Berlin-Amsterdam)
- **Country:** Finland (group core markets: Finland, Germany, Austria, Netherlands)
- **Licence:** ECSP licence held at group level via Austrian FMA (first ECSP licence in the DACH region), passported into Finland; Finnish site confirms 'EU:n ECSP-asetuksen mukainen toimilupa'. Finnish-founded (2012), merged with Finnest, Kapilendo, Oneplanetcrowd.
- **Model:** Equity + debt crowdfunding (shares, bonds, convertible loans); generalist growth companies and SMEs, incl. pre-IPO bond products
- **Instruments:** Real direct shares (equity), bonds/joukkovelkakirjat (e.g. 8.75% p.a. coupons), convertible loans. No SPV/nominee layer highlighted for Finnish equity deals.
- **Fees:** Investors: ~1.5% transaction fee on equity campaigns, 0 on bonds; min ticket €250 (marketed 'from €250', some sources €80 historic). Project owners: listing + success fee (not published precisely; historically ~6% success + fixed listing).
- **Volumes:** Group cumulative €614M raised, 1,076 projects, 209,000 registered investors (invesdor.fi, checked Aug 2026; €590M mid-2025 → ~€600M by end-2025). Lean org (~15 employees per Tracxn Apr 2026). New Nordics head appointed Dec 2025 — active Nordic push.
- **Strengths:** Only true pan-European equity CSP active in Finland; single ECSPR passport across FI/DE/AT/NL/BE; Largest investor base in the region (209k registered); Multi-instrument (equity + bonds + convertibles) smooths deal flow; Strong brand heritage in Finland (invented Finnish equity crowdfunding, 2012)
- **Weaknesses:** Strategic centre of gravity shifted to DACH; Finland is now a satellite market with thin local team (~15 staff group-wide); Charges investors 1.5% on equity — vulnerable to a 0-fee challenger; No secondary market / no bulletin board visible; Deal curation reputation diluted across 4 markets; group cumulative numbers mask modest Finnish equity run-rate
- **Notes:** The benchmark passporting case: licence in one NCA (FMA Austria), sales across EU. Finnish origin story + FMA licensing shows regulator arbitrage is normal in this market. No Baltic project-owner activity found. Growth via M&A (Finnest, Kapilendo, Oneplanetcrowd) rather than organic.

### Springvest Oyj
- **Country:** Finland
- **Licence:** NOT an ECSP. Springvest is a Finnish MiFID investment firm authorised by FIN-FSA for order transmission, investment advice and arrangement of issue placements. This lets it run rounds far above the ECSPR €5M cap (advertises €5–50M rounds) but it has no ECSPR passport. Springvest Oyj itself is listed on Nasdaq First North Helsinki.
- **Model:** Equity growth-funding platform for Finnish deep-tech/technology growth companies; NOT a typical crowdfunding site — hybrid retail + institutional placement house
- **Instruments:** Real direct shares in the target company (unlisted Finnish Oy shares); no SPV/nominee. Springvest additionally takes options/warrants in funded companies (sold later for its own P&L).
- **Fees:** INVESTORS PAY: 3% subscription fee early in each round, 4% later. Companies pay cash commission + options: FY2025 income €5.9M fees/commissions + €6.7M option income against €47M arranged (+€41M co-arranged) — an effective all-in take rate well above 10% when options are counted. Min ticket varies per round (typically ~€500).
- **Volumes:** €428M cumulative arranged, 110 rounds, 69 companies, 16,974 registered investors, 100% of rounds successfully closed (springvest.fi, Aug 2026). 2025: €47M arranged + €41M co-arranged; 8–10 public offerings/year out of ~200 companies screened.
- **Strengths:** Best deal quality signal in the Nordics: 100% round completion, heavy curation (~5% acceptance); Institutional/family-office co-investment model (2025) stacks retail on top of anchor money; Can do €5–50M rounds — outside ECSPR's reach; Public company transparency builds trust
- **Weaknesses:** Charges investors 3–4% — the single most attackable feature in the Finnish market; Finland-only; no passport, no Baltic presence, Finnish-language ecosystem; Only 8–10 deals/year — starves smaller SMEs (the client's €150–800k segment is below Springvest's radar); Option-fee model creates conflict-of-interest optics (platform profits from valuation, not investor outcome); 12 exits vs 12 write-offs — returns story unproven
- **Notes:** Springvest deliberately vacated the sub-€1M SME segment the client targets. Its investor base (17k Finns paying 3–4% fees) is the exact pool a 0-fee ECSPR passporting entrant would poach. Its non-ECSPR MiFID route is the structural opposite of the client's plan.

### Vauraus Suomi Oyj
- **Country:** Finland
- **Licence:** First and (at authorisation) only Finnish-licensed ECSP — FIN-FSA authorisation 24 Oct 2023. Backed by a €27.5M EIF cornerstone investment into its SME loan fund; tripartite securitisation setup with Chartered Investment and Exaloan.
- **Model:** SME crowdlending (business loans from €100) — NOT equity; included as the licensing datapoint
- **Instruments:** Intermediated business loans only
- **Fees:** Lending-market pricing; not relevant to equity comparison
- **Volumes:** Not disclosed for 2025; est. ~150+ SMEs via EIF-backed fund
- **Strengths:** Shows FIN-FSA is willing and able to license ECSPs; EIF endorsement of Finnish crowdfunding
- **Weaknesses:** No equity capability — leaves Finnish ECSPR equity space effectively EMPTY of domestic licensees
- **Notes:** Key regulatory fact: Finland's domestic ECSP roster is essentially one lending platform. Finnish equity crowdfunding is served by a foreign-licensed passport (Invesdor/FMA) and a MiFID firm (Springvest) — an Estonian ECSP passporting in would not face a domestically-licensed equity CSP.

### FundedByMe / Pepins Group (Sweden) — DEFUNCT
- **Country:** Sweden (Nordic passporting reference)
- **Licence:** Defunct. FundedByMe merged with Pepins (2021), renamed Pepins Group Feb 2022; Pepins announced discontinuation of operations Feb 2025. ~470 companies funded, >€52M lifetime.
- **Model:** Equity crowdfunding (was the main Nordic cross-border player, incl. Finland activity)
- **Instruments:** Equity (historically direct + nominee structures)
- **Fees:** n/a (closed)
- **Volumes:** €52M+ lifetime across 470 companies from 25 countries — over ~14 years, i.e. structurally sub-scale
- **Weaknesses:** Proof that generalist, low-curation Nordic equity crowdfunding without a clear fee/quality edge dies: sub-scale volumes, listed-company cost base, no profitable niche
- **Notes:** The Nordic cautionary tale. Sweden now has no meaningful equity-CSP champion; no Nordic platform is actively passporting into the Baltics as of Aug 2026 — the 'Nordic invader' threat is currently theoretical, not real.

### CrowdedHero (SIA CrowdedHero Latvia)
- **Country:** Latvia
- **Licence:** Latvia's FIRST ECSP authorisation — licence 06.15.01.806/120, issued 16 Aug 2022 (by FCMC, now supervised by Latvijas Banka). Actively passporting deals from Spain; references to Finland, Poland, Romania on site.
- **Model:** Equity crowdfunding + fixed-income (bonds) + some lending projects; verticals: fintech (Simpleros, Spain), manor-restoration real-estate equity, SME growth
- **Instruments:** Real equity (share capital) AND an 'indirect' SPV model depending on project; digital fixed-yield bonds (e.g. 10.5%); secondary market module exists on-site
- **Fees:** INVESTORS PAY THREE LAYERS: 1.25% admin fee per investment, 5.5% fee on dividends (from year 2), 9% carry on capital gains at exit. Min ticket €100. Registration free. Project-owner fees not published.
- **Volumes:** Very small: ~€600k raised for a handful of companies by early 2025 (p2pempire); current live projects show €7–9k raised per open campaign (site, Aug 2026). Investor count not published.
- **Strengths:** 4-year head start on Latvian equity licence; Secondary market feature already built; Multi-country deal sourcing (Spain, Baltics); Low €100 minimum
- **Weaknesses:** Practically no traction — ~€600k in 3 years is a failed flywheel; Triple-layer investor fees (1.25% + 5.5% + 9% carry) vs client's €0 investor model; Mixed equity/bonds/lending identity confuses positioning; No visible investor base or repeat-deal cadence
- **Notes:** The only real equity CSP in the Baltics — and it is tiny. Its fee stack is the clearest competitive gift to a 0-investor-fee entrant. Latvia keeps licensing new CSPs (Fundaus 3/2025, Ascend Finance 7/2025, FLOW 8/2025, Digilo 9/2025, CSP Growth Solutions) but ALL new ones are lending-only.

### Capitalia SE
- **Country:** Latvia (pan-Baltic operations)
- **Licence:** ECSP authorisation from Latvijas Banka 1 Nov 2023 (Capitalia SE as platform operator; post-licence the operator itself may no longer invest in loans — the holding does)
- **Model:** SME business lending / crowdloans — NOT equity (holding company separately runs a co-investment fund that takes equity stakes, e.g. Cenos, Xolo, Aispeco); novelty: crypto-collateralised business loans (2025)
- **Instruments:** Business loans; equity only via its off-platform co-investment fund
- **Fees:** Lending-market pricing; not published in comparable equity terms
- **Volumes:** Operating since 2007; >5,000 businesses financed, >€100M deployed lifetime (pan-Baltic). FY2025: turnover €1.89M, loss €88k, portfolio +10% y/y — profitable-scale it is not.
- **Strengths:** Deepest Baltic SME origination network and credit data (ES/LV/LT); Brand trust with exactly the client's target company profile (established SMEs, €0.5–3M revenue)
- **Weaknesses:** No equity crowdfunding product on-platform; Small revenue base, loss-making 2025; Lending economics cap its ability to fund expansion
- **Notes:** Not an equity competitor but the most credible potential one: it already originates the client's target SMEs across all three Baltics and could bolt equity onto its ECSP licence. Watch item, and possibly a partnership/acquisition candidate for Baltic deal flow.

### FinBee Verslui (Finbee)
- **Country:** Lithuania
- **Licence:** ECSP-licensed via Bank of Lithuania (among the 12 CSPs re-licensed under ECSPR in 2023)
- **Model:** SME lending crowdfunding (P2B); consumer P2P via sister entity — NOT equity ('Seedrs of Lithuania' does not exist; FinBee is the closest SME-finance brand)
- **Instruments:** Business loans only; no shares, no securities
- **Fees:** Borrower interest + platform margin; no equity-comparable fee schedule
- **Volumes:** Raised €35M institutional funding in July 2024 to accelerate SME lending (target: 1,500+ businesses); large-loan crowdfunding now completes in hours
- **Strengths:** Fast, automated SME underwriting; Strong Lithuanian SME brand and repeat borrowers
- **Weaknesses:** Zero equity capability or stated ambition — moving toward balance-sheet-style lending, away from crowd model
- **Notes:** Confirms the pattern: every scaled Lithuanian CSP is debt. FinBee's SME borrower base (established, revenue-generating companies) is demographically identical to the client's target project owners — those SMEs currently have NO domestic equity option.

### Profitus (UAB Profitus Crowdfunding) — Lithuanian market-scale reference
- **Country:** Lithuania
- **Licence:** ECSP licence from Bank of Lithuania 10 Nov 2023
- **Model:** Real-estate-secured lending crowdfunding — NOT equity; included to size Lithuanian retail crowd-investing demand
- **Instruments:** Secured loans
- **Fees:** Investor-side largely free; borrower pays; min ticket from ~€100 (market entry tickets in LT go as low as €10 on some platforms)
- **Volumes:** €273M+ cumulative; €98M in the 12 months to mid-2025 (+35.5% y/y, ~€8.2M/month); 1,655 projects; 10,000+ active investors (2026). Whole Lithuanian CSP market 2024: >500,000 transactions, €278.4M.
- **Strengths:** Proves large, fast-growing Lithuanian retail crowd-investor pool with mobile-first UX expectations
- **Weaknesses:** Not a competitor for equity; sets a high UX/velocity bar (hours-to-fund) that an equity entrant will be measured against
- **Notes:** Lithuania's crowd-investing demand (~€278M/yr) is entirely channelled into debt because no domestic equity product exists. Letsinvest (RE CSP, ECSP LB002206 since 25 Jul 2023) had its licence EXPANDED in April 2025 to include securities placement and SPV operations — the first Lithuanian CSP inching toward securities-based offerings; watch item.

### Republic Europe (formerly Seedrs)
- **Country:** UK / Ireland (pan-EU passport)
- **Licence:** ECSP authorised via Central Bank of Ireland since Oct 2023 (Dublin EU HQ), passportable to all EU incl. FI/LV/LT; also FCA-regulated in the UK (FRN 550317)
- **Model:** Equity crowdfunding, generalist tech/consumer; nominee structure; large secondary market
- **Instruments:** Shares held via Republic/Seedrs NOMINEE (not direct title) — contrast with client's register-of-record direct ownership
- **Fees:** Investor: free to invest historically, but 1.5% (capped) transaction/exit-type fees and secondary-market spreads; company: ~6% + completion costs (UK schedule; EU schedule similar)
- **Volumes:** ~£3B raised over ~2,400 rounds by 2025 (global); secondary market moved to 24/7/365 in 2025 — £1M traded in May 2025 alone
- **Strengths:** Scale, brand, and the deepest retail secondary market in European equity crowdfunding; One Irish licence serves the whole EU — the model the client is replicating from Estonia
- **Weaknesses:** No visible Baltic/Finnish deal-sourcing or localisation; EU activity concentrated in Western Europe; Nominee custody model is heavier and less transparent than Estonian direct-register ownership
- **Notes:** The latent pan-EU threat rather than a present Baltic competitor. Its 24/7 secondary market shows where investor expectations are heading; ECSPR art 25 bulletin board (client's plan) is the licence-light answer.

### Funderbeam (Venturebeam Markets AS)
- **Country:** Estonia origin; EEA clients served from Tallinn, non-EEA from London, plus Singapore entity
- **Licence:** Operates as an INVESTMENT FIRM licensed by Estonian FSA (Venturebeam Markets AS) — not an ECSP; serves 130 countries
- **Model:** Private-company funding + exchange-like SECONDARY trading marketplace (auto-match, 24/7, T+10min settlement); pivoted from retail crowdfunding toward syndication/trading venue
- **Instruments:** Tradeable interests in private companies via marketplace structures (not direct Business-Register share title)
- **Fees:** Trading/syndication fees; primary-raise fees not standardised publicly
- **Volumes:** Not disclosed recently; niche scale
- **Strengths:** Only functioning private-market secondary venue born in the Baltics; proof Estonian regulators support innovative structures
- **Weaknesses:** Abandoned the retail primary-crowdfunding lane the client targets; Complex structure vs client's simple direct-ownership story
- **Notes:** Not a passporting equity CSP competitor, but a home-market reference: its exit from retail primary raises leaves Estonian/Baltic equity crowdfunding open.
