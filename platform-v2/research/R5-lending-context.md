# Baltic lending platforms — the wallet competitors

## Strategic insights

- Pool size, honestly measured: the 19 major Baltic-linked P2P platforms report ~EUR 20.9B cumulative funding and 1.3M+ registered investor accounts (13 of 19 HQ'd in the Baltics), but those bases are pan-European (Germany/Spain-heavy) and overlapping — the same person holds Mintos+Bondora+EstateGuru accounts. Realistic Baltic-RESIDENT retail alternative-investor pool: Profitus's ~49k (LT), Bondora's Estonian slice, EstateGuru's local core — roughly 100-300k active people region-wide, with EU crowdfunding average tickets of ~EUR 660 and 88% retail share. Estonia-first TAM is tens of thousands of investors, not hundreds of thousands; pan-EU passporting (art 18) is where the real wallet is.
- Registered-investor counts are vanity metrics — money-in-motion is the real gauge: Bondora attracts EUR 40-45M/month of retail inflows at just 6% flat, and Profitus funds EUR 5.5M/month in one small country. The retail wallet exists and is habitual; the binding constraint is distribution and trust, not appetite.
- The EstateGuru lesson on trust: collateral marketing does not survive bad underwriting. 'First-rank mortgage secured' loans still hit >60% of managed portfolio in recovery, with 3-5 year workout timelines. For the client this cuts both ways — never oversell the safety of OÜ equity, and make the risk architecture (title in the Business Register, survives platform insolvency) the honest headline instead of implied downside protection.
- The second EstateGuru lesson: cross-border expansion before underwriting maturity is what killed it (Germany 2020-21 origination, now EUR 95.7M stuck in exited markets), while its home Baltic book performed. The client's Estonia-first, passport-later sequencing is validated by the market's biggest failure — say so in the FI application and to investors.
- Fee treatment of trapped investors is a reputational landmine: EstateGuru's EUR 100/month inactivity penalty and Mintos's EUR 4.90/month no-floor inactivity fee are the most-cited grievances in 2026 reviews. The client's EUR 0-investor-fee promise is a genuine, provable differentiator — but it must be permanent and contractual, because fee creep after trouble is the pattern retail investors have now learned to fear.
- Communication cadence is a survival technology: Bondora (17 years of monthly stats posts) and Profitus (monthly funding reports) kept trust through cycles; EstateGuru's recovery updates, though grim, are the only thing keeping it alive; platforms that went quiet died. Budget for radical, scheduled transparency — monthly portfolio stats, per-offer outcome reporting — as a core product feature from day one.
- Buyback/guarantee structures concentrate rather than remove risk: Income and Debitum's protections are only as good as originator solvency (the mechanism that failed on Mintos in 2020). Equity has no equivalent false comfort to sell — position that as honesty: 'we never promise your money back; we give you real ownership and real disclosure (KIIS, audited accounts, 4-day withdrawal right)'.
- How to position equity against 8-11% (up to 14%) 'secured' interest: do not fight on headline yield — reframe the comparison. Lending caps the upside at the coupon while EstateGuru proved the downside tail is real and illiquid for years; equity in a 3+ year-old revenue-generating OÜ offers uncapped upside, dividends, and legally registered ownership. Target the diversification euro of the existing 100-300k P2P-literate investors — 'you already have loans; this is the ownership sleeve of your portfolio' — rather than trying to convert deposit savers first.
- Regulatory positioning is now a marketing asset in this market: Mintos rebuilt itself by moving to MiFID Notes, Crowdpear leads with its ECSP licence and ISO 27001, while Bondora's flagship 6% product remains formally unprotected. An ECSPR licence from Finantsinspektsioon plus MiFID-grade disclosure habits lets the client credibly claim MORE formal investor protection than Estonia's largest retail investment product (Go & Grow) — a striking, true claim for Estonian marketing.
- Liquidity expectations are set high (Bondora: instant; Mintos: 0.85% secondary exit) and Debitum shows a no-liquidity regulated product stays niche. The client's art 25 bulletin board will disappoint P2P-trained users unless expectations are explicitly reset at onboarding: frame equity as a 3-7 year hold, make the bulletin board's non-binding reference price and the digital transfer rail (the notary-waiver articles) the story — 'transferable when you find a buyer, digitally, in minutes' — not 'liquid'.

## Platforms

### EstateGuru
- **Country:** Estonia (HQ Tallinn; loans historically in EE/LV/LT/FI/DE/ES/PT)
- **Licence:** ECSP licence from Finantsinspektsioon since May 2023 (decision no. 4.1-1/48) — still active and verifiable in the FI register; the same regulator the client is applying to
- **Model:** Lending — property-backed bridge/development/business loans secured by first-rank mortgages; retail + (formerly) institutional co-funding
- **Fees:** Borrowers pay origination fees. Investors (post-2023 fee creep): EUR 3 per withdrawal, 1% secondary-market seller fee, 0.083%/month (~1%/yr) AUM fee on performing principal, inactivity penalty escalated to EUR 10 then EUR 50/month and EUR 100/month from 01.11.2025
- **Volumes:** ~EUR 939M funded cumulatively across 7,744 deals; 159,563 investors from 106 countries (April 2026). New issuance collapsed: EUR 246.5M total 2023-2025, only EUR 18.6M issued in 2026 YTD
- **Strengths:** Strongest brand recognition in Baltic property lending; first-mover since 2014; Real mortgage collateral and an ECSPR passport across the EU; Still recovering money for investors (EUR 70M principal recovered to date) and publishing detailed per-market recovery updates
- **Weaknesses:** Catastrophic default legacy: at Aug 2025 EUR 133.6M in recovery = >60% of managed portfolio; still EUR 32.5M in recovery in active markets + EUR 95.7M in exited markets (Germany, Finland); German book blow-up (2023): 76% of EUR 85.2M late/defaulted, internal probe into improper loan origination 2020-2021, 3-5 year recovery timelines, ~EUR 1M/yr legal costs; Institutional funding share collapsed from ~30% to 5% — smart money left first; Fee creep on captive investors (EUR 100/month inactivity penalty) is widely cited in reviews as trust-destroying
- **Notes:** The cautionary tale of the market. Lessons: 'secured by mortgage' marketing did not survive bad underwriting and cross-border expansion (Germany/Finland killed it, home Baltics performed); recovery on defaulted secured loans takes 3-5 years, and Finland's 30-year-high bankruptcy wave (3,800+ corporate bankruptcies in 2025) keeps dragging it; monetising trapped investors with penalty fees compounded reputational damage. Sources: estateguru.co/blog recovery updates mid-2026, fi.ee licence notice, p2pempire/rethink-p2p 2026 reviews.

### Bondora / Go & Grow
- **Country:** Estonia (lending markets: Finland 68.6%, Netherlands 12.6%, Estonia 11.8%, Denmark, Latvia — Feb 2026)
- **Licence:** NOT ECSPR. Bondora AS is a licensed consumer credit provider supervised by Finantsinspektsioon; the retail product entity (Bondora Capital OÜ renamed Go&Grow OÜ, reg. 12831506, April 2026, spun to its own goandgrow.eu domain) holds no product-specific licence — investors have no MiFID/ECSPR/deposit protection
- **Model:** Unsecured consumer lending funded by retail via Go & Grow, a flat-6% instant-liquidity savings-like product (moved to flat 6%, no tiers, in 2025)
- **Fees:** EUR 0 investor fees except EUR 1 per withdrawal; Bondora keeps the entire spread above the flat 6% paid out
- **Volumes:** 500,000+ registered investors (milestone passed Nov 2025) — the largest Estonian retail base by far; ~EUR 31-32M/month loan originations; EUR 40-45M/month inflows into Go & Grow; EUR 169M+ total investor earnings over 17 years; Group 2025: revenue EUR 62.7M (+19%), net profit EUR 9.5M (vs EUR 1.2M 2024), KPMG-audited
- **Strengths:** Half a million registered investors and frictionless one-click UX — proof of how big the passive euro-retail wallet is; Genuinely profitable and growing (rare in the sector); 17 years of monthly statistics blogging = communication discipline that carried it through 2008- and 2020-era drawdowns
- **Weaknesses:** 6% flat return now sits below money-market funds and bank term deposits at times — value proposition eroding; Unregulated product wrapper; withdrawal gates were imposed in 2020 (liquidity is 'usually' instant, not guaranteed); Yield cap means all upside above 6% accrues to Bondora, not investors — the anti-model of equity alignment
- **Notes:** Strategically the most instructive for sizing: it shows retail will hand over EUR 40M+/month for a simple, boring 6% if onboarding is effortless. The April 2026 Bondora/Go&Grow corporate split is worth watching — reviewers flag it as regulatory-perimeter management. An ECSPR equity platform can honestly say it offers MORE formal investor protection (KIIS, reflection period, regulated provider) than Estonia's biggest retail investment product.

### Mintos
- **Country:** Latvia (Riga)
- **Licence:** NOT ECSPR — chose the MiFID route: MiFID II investment-firm licence no. 06.06.08.719/534 + e-money institution licence, both from Latvijas Banka (originally FCMC, Aug 2021). National investor-compensation scheme: 90% of net loss up to EUR 20,000
- **Model:** Loan-notes marketplace (loans repackaged as MiFID II asset-backed securities called Notes) + deliberate multi-asset expansion: bonds (40+ European issuers incl. airBaltic, Eleving, Summus), ETFs, fractional real estate, Smart Cash MMF, and regulated crypto ETPs from March 2026 (with Upvest) — repositioning as a mass-retail wealth platform ('Europe's everything-app for yield')
- **Fees:** EUR 0 to deposit/invest in loans; 0.85% secondary-market seller fee; EUR 4.90/month inactivity fee after 360 days of no activity (no minimum-balance threshold); bond portfolios 0.39%/yr management fee from 2026
- **Volumes:** EUR 12B loans funded since 2015; ~EUR 600M assets currently under administration; 700,000+ registered investors (April 2026) — Europe's largest marketplace-lending platform. Bonds: +61% investors and +86% volume 2024→2025
- **Strengths:** Scale and brand: 700k investors, pan-EU distribution, full regulated-instruments stack; Survived its own 2020 crisis (multiple loan-originator defaults, ~EUR 100M+ tied up in recoveries at peak) and rebuilt by moving from unregulated claim-rights to regulated Notes; Product velocity: bonds, ETFs, MMF, crypto ETPs in ~3 years
- **Weaknesses:** Legacy recoveries from 2020 originator defaults still not fully resolved — long memory among Baltic/German investor communities; Increasing complexity; core loan yields compressed as it chases the mainstream neobroker segment; Inactivity fee with no balance floor irritates small dormant accounts
- **Notes:** Mintos is the template for 'regulate up to rebuild trust': its 2021 pivot from claim-rights to MiFID Notes is the lending-side analogue of the client's cap-table/Business-Register title argument. It is also becoming wallet-share competition on every asset class at once — an equity ECSPR platform will be fighting Mintos for the same 'diversify beyond loans' euro.

### Income Marketplace
- **Country:** Estonia (Tallinn), launched 2021; loans from originators in Indonesia, Latvia, Bulgaria, etc.
- **Licence:** Not ECSPR, not MiFID — unregulated claim-rights model (consumer-loan marketplaces fall outside ECSPR scope); relies on structural protections instead of a licence
- **Model:** Consumer/SME loan marketplace (claim rights, not securities) with three-layer protection: 60-day buyback obligation + originator cashflow buffer + junior-share skin-in-the-game
- **Fees:** EUR 0 investor fees
- **Volumes:** ~EUR 146M cumulatively funded; ~9,100-10,000 investors; 97.8% of portfolio performing, 2.2% in recovery; zero investor capital losses to date; advertised average return 13.78% (range 8-15%)
- **Strengths:** Clean loss record and genuinely thoughtful security architecture; High headline yields (13%+) keep it attractive to yield-hunters
- **Weaknesses:** Small and not yet profitable — breakeven was targeted for end-2025 at ~EUR 30M AUM and reviewers still flag platform-viability risk in 2026; Buyback protection is only as good as originator solvency (the exact mechanism that failed investors on Mintos in 2020); No licence = no regulatory backstop; ~9-10k investors after 5 years shows how hard organic retail acquisition is without a big brand
- **Notes:** Useful benchmark for the client's fundraising narrative: a well-run unregulated platform took 5 years to reach ~10k investors and EUR 146M. Regulation + zero-fee positioning is a differentiator, but distribution is the real bottleneck.

### Crowdpear
- **Country:** Lithuania (Vilnius); projects in Lithuania and Romania
- **Licence:** ECSP licence from the Bank of Lithuania since July 2023; first Lithuanian crowdfunding platform with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certification (Dec 2025)
- **Model:** ECSPR lending-based crowdfunding — real-estate-mortgage-secured business loans; sister platform of PeerBerry (same team/owners), built explicitly to give PeerBerry's investor base a regulated product
- **Fees:** EUR 0 investor fees; project owners pay listing/success fees (not publicly itemised)
- **Volumes:** ~EUR 42.5M funded since Jan 2023 launch; zero confirmed capital losses; 9.26% of historical volume has hit 90+ days late; 3 projects (EUR 496,646) in active recovery early 2026; median investor XIRR ~11.9%; yields 10.5-11.5% typical (up to 14 percent), min ticket EUR 100, quarterly interest
- **Strengths:** ECSPR-licensed with a clean record and strong per-project disclosure (LTV, valuation, docs) — closest operating example of the disclosure standard the client will meet; Can cross-sell PeerBerry's large existing investor community
- **Weaknesses:** Modest volumes after 3 years despite a famous parent brand — regulated real-estate lending in LT is a crowded niche; Geographic concentration (LT + Romania); rising 90+ day late share bears watching
- **Notes:** Direct evidence that an ECSP licence alone does not create volume: EUR 42.5M in 3 years even with PeerBerry's distribution. Its ISO 27001 move shows Baltic platforms now compete on institutional-grade trust signals.

### Profitus
- **Country:** Lithuania (Vilnius), operating since Aug 2018; expanding into Latvia/Estonia/Spain
- **Licence:** ECSP licence since November 2023, supervised by the Bank of Lithuania (regulated by LB since inception under the older LT national regime)
- **Model:** ECSPR lending-based crowdfunding — real-estate-mortgage-backed business/development loans; market leader in Lithuania
- **Fees:** Investors: free (small claim-transfer fee only on secondary sales). Borrowers: 0.5-8% of loan amount + 0.1-0.2%/month administration fee
- **Volumes:** EUR 300-350M+ funded cumulatively; 49,000+ registered investors (largely Lithuanian retail); run-rate ~EUR 5-5.5M/month across ~28 loans (June 2026); average return ~11%, up to 14%; in-recovery 2.59% + late 0.36%; only 2 of first 1,000 loans went to recovery; zero investor capital losses
- **Strengths:** Best trust record among Baltic property-lending platforms: <5% lifetime default share, zero losses, 49k investors; Shows a single-country retail base of ~49k active alternative investors exists in Lithuania alone; Success-fee-on-borrower model closely parallels the client's fee design
- **Weaknesses:** Deep concentration in the Lithuanian real-estate cycle; Yield expectations it sets (11-14% secured) are the hardest comp for equity products to argue against
- **Notes:** Profitus is the healthy mirror-image of EstateGuru: same asset class, disciplined single-market underwriting, radically better outcomes. Its 49k mostly-domestic investors are the best proxy for how many retail alternative investors one Baltic country can supply.

### Debitum
- **Country:** Latvia (Riga), launched Sept 2018
- **Licence:** Investment brokerage firm (IBF) licence since 2021, supervised by Latvijas Banka (ex-FCMC); MiFID framework, investor compensation scheme 90% up to EUR 20,000 — like Mintos, chose MiFID over ECSPR
- **Model:** SME business-loan investing via regulated asset-backed securities (bundled notes); originators in Estonia, Latvia, UK; 90-day buyback + 15% penalty on originator delays
- **Fees:** EUR 0 investor fees; no secondary market at all (buy-and-hold to maturity)
- **Volumes:** Niche scale (order of tens of EUR millions funded; investor base in the low tens of thousands — exact current figures not published); returns 8-16% advertised, 9-13% typically realised; stated 0% investor loss record; first profitable year 2025
- **Strengths:** Fully regulated instruments at boutique scale; first profitability reached 2025; Clean loss record and structured 4-step originator due diligence
- **Weaknesses:** No secondary market = zero liquidity, undermining the 'notes are proper securities' story; Small and dependent on a handful of loan originators (concentration risk reviewers consistently flag)
- **Notes:** Demonstrates that a regulated wrapper without liquidity or scale stays niche. Its 15%-penalty buyback design is a good example of aligning originator incentives contractually — analogous thinking could inform the client's project-owner covenants.
