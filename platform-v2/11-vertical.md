RECOMMENDATION: [VERTIKAAL] = vertical-flavoured generalist — "Estonian real-economy growth companies" (consumer brands + manufacturing/green industry as the flavour), not a hard vertical.

**Why a hard vertical fails at this market size.** Sustainability needs ~15–30 closed deals/yr (EUR 9–48k revenue per deal at 6%). No single Estonian vertical produces 15+ qualifying companies (3+ yrs, EUR 0.5–3M revenue, willing to sell 5–15%) per year — a hard vertical caps the platform below Crowdestate's death-line. But pure horizontality is what killed FundedByMe/Pepins (generalist low-curation) and stalls CrowdedHero. The Springvest lesson is curation discipline, not sector narrowness.

**Scorecard (deal flow / retail appeal / economics / regulatory / passporting, 1–5):**

(a) **Horizontal generalist — 5/3/4/5/4.** Only option that mathematically feeds the P&L. Weakness: no brand identity, adverse-selection risk. Fix with flavour, below.

(b) **F&B & consumer brands — 3/5/3/5/2.** Best community-investor mechanics in crowdfunding anywhere: customers become shareholders, issuer markets its own raise to its base (the art 27-compliant channel). Real pipeline exists: La Muu (already crowdfunded twice), Põhjala brewery, Karma Kombucha, ÖÖD houses, Huum and Ampler Bikes (both proved Estonian consumer-brand raises on Funderbeam). Weakness: rounds skew small (EUR 150–300k → EUR 9–18k fees), maybe 4–8 deals/yr, and Estonian brands don't travel to FI/LV/LT.

(c) **Manufacturing/industrial incl. timber & green transition — 4/3/5/5/5.** Estonia's deepest stock of exactly-profile companies: timber/modular construction (Timbeco, Kodasema, Harmet-tier suppliers), metal/machinery subcontractors, green-transition retooling. Larger rounds (EUR 400–800k → EUR 24–48k fees), larger tickets, dividend-capable. Strong passport story — industrial SMEs are the common language of LV/LT/FI economies. Weakness: less viral retail appeal; needs the consumer layer for audience-building.

(d) **Defence & dual-use — 2/4/3/1/3.** Hot (Milrem, Frankenburg, DefSecIntel, KrattWorks show the ecosystem), but wrong for this platform: the best companies are VC-funded at bigger tickets; genuinely investable ones are often pre-revenue (excluded by the 3-yr/revenue filter); weapons are on the client's own exclusion list, and the dual-use boundary creates export-control questions, PSP refusal risk (many payment processors won't touch defence — a direct art 10(5) operational risk), and FI-application complexity for zero precedent benefit. Verdict: allow defence-*adjacent* civilian-revenue companies (sensors, logistics software, textiles with MoD contracts) case-by-case under the generalist umbrella; never market it as a vertical; keep "weapons/ammunition" exclusion verbatim.

(e) **Renewables & cleantech — 3/4/4/2/4.** Retail appetite proven (Enefit Green, 60k subscribers). But the natural deal shape — single-project SPVs — is precisely the AIFMD passive-vehicle trap and the WiSEED failure pattern already excluded in the programme of operations. Only *operating* companies qualify (Roofit.Solar-type manufacturers, Bisly-type building tech, installers with 3-yr track records) — a thin sub-segment. Treat as a sub-flavour of (c), never a standalone.

(f) **Tourism/hospitality — 2/2/2/4/1.** Seasonal, low-margin, asset-heavy (drifts toward real-estate holdcos — exclusion tension), post-EstateGuru association with Baltic property risk, no passport story. Reject as a focus; accept exceptional cases.

(g) **Tech/SaaS scale-ups — 2/4/3/4/4.** Adverse-selection problem: Estonian SaaS at EUR 0.5–3M revenue with real growth raises VC; crowdfunding signals "VC passed". Valuation fights, no dividends, and it collides with Crowdcube/Seedrs' passported home turf. Take profitable, dividend-capable niche software case-by-case; don't lead with it.

**Recommended structure.** Position as "the ownership platform for Estonia's real economy — companies you can touch, taste and visit as a shareholder": a curated generalist with two flavour pillars — consumer brands (audience engine: many small tickets, community virality, launch anchor deals from the Fundwise/Funderbeam alumni pool) and manufacturing/green industry (revenue engine: bigger rounds, bigger tickets, dividend narratives). Sequence: launch with 1–2 consumer-brand offers for visible oversubscription, add industrial deals by month 6, open renewables-operating-company and defence-adjacent sub-tracks opportunistically. For passporting, lead with the industrial/green-transition pillar (travels; Springvest/Invesdor leave EUR 150–800k unserved) while using Estonian consumer deals as export product to Finnish/Baltic retail.

**For the FI application:** state the focus as "established Estonian operating companies across consumer and industrial sectors", with the existing exclusion list unchanged — this reads as risk management (diversification across sectors, revenue-generating issuers only), whereas a hard vertical would read as concentration risk.
